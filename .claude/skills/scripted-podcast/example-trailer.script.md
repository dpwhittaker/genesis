## Cold open — "the Lord regretted"

TEACHER: [quiet, measured] "The Lord regretted that he had made humankind on the earth."

STUDENT: [surprised] Wait. God *regretted*?

TEACHER: Genesis six, verse six. The Jews who first put Genesis into Greek
couldn't quite say it. In their version, God… "pondered it deeply."

STUDENT: [laughs] So they softened it.

TEACHER: They had their reasons. In Numbers, God is not a man, "that he should
change his mind."

STUDENT: [incredulous] So the Bible says both?

TEACHER: Sometimes in one chapter. First Samuel fifteen: the Lord regrets making
Saul king; he does not change his mind; he regrets it again.

STUDENT: [curious] So how do people hold that together?

TEACHER: In about five ways. And serious believers hold every one of them.

## Whose fault — verse five

STUDENT: [thoughtful] But why was God sorry at all? Last session it was the sons
of God, the Nephilim…

TEACHER: And then verse five changes the subject. Humankind. "Every inclination
of the thoughts of their minds was only evil all the time."

STUDENT: [sighs] So no "the serpent tricked me."

TEACHER: Not this time. And after the flood, God says almost the same words
again, only now they're the reason for mercy.

STUDENT: [softly] Because the water can wash the earth…

TEACHER: [warmly] …but not the human heart.

## Grace — verse eight

STUDENT: Please tell me there's good news in here.

TEACHER: Verse eight. "But Noah found favor in the sight of the Lord." It's the
first time the Bible uses its word for grace. And Noah's name, in Hebrew, is
that word spelled backward.

STUDENT: [delighted] No way.

---

## A story told twice

TEACHER: Then keep reading, and the story starts over.

STUDENT: [curious] Over?

TEACHER: Two of every animal; then seven pairs of the clean ones. "The Lord";
then "God."

STUDENT: [skeptical] So… two writers?

TEACHER: Maybe. Two strands, woven together. Or one story built like a mirror,
with "God remembered Noah" at the center. Or the oldest answer: one
Author, and nothing said twice for nothing.

STUDENT: [excited] So which is it?

TEACHER: [chuckles] We'll lay out all three, each at its strongest. You may hold
any of them.

## Close

STUDENT: Session nine: Regret, Grace, and a Story Told Twice. The handout and
two longer conversations are on the class site.

TEACHER: Bring your questions. Genesis six has plenty of its own.
