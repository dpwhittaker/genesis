# Genesis Through Ancient Eyes

A small-group curriculum that walks through the book of Genesis as its first Hebrew hearers and ancient Jewish readers would have understood it.

## Why read Genesis this way

Genesis was not written to us, but it was written *for* us. Its first audience lived in the world of the Ancient Near East — surrounded by Babylonian, Canaanite, and Egyptian stories of gods, kings, temples, and floods. They read in Hebrew, heard its wordplay, and felt the weight of covenant, blessing, land, and seed.

This study slows down to ask, at every turn:

- **What did the Hebrew actually say?** Word choices, puns, and names carry meaning lost in translation (`adam` / `adamah`, `Babel` / `balal`, `Yitzchaq` / *he laughs*).
- **What world did the first hearers live in?** We compare Genesis to *Enuma Elish*, the *Atrahasis* and *Gilgamesh* flood accounts, and ANE temple and covenant patterns — to see where Genesis echoes its neighbors and where it deliberately breaks from them.
- **How did ancient Jews read it?** We listen to Second Temple readings, the Targums, and early rabbinic interpretation alongside the New Testament's own use of Genesis.
- **Why does it matter now?** Each session lands on what the text claimed about God, humanity, and the world — and what it still claims.

Many of us were trained to read Genesis as if it were a modern science-or-history textbook. So this study deliberately starts *slowly*, and not at "day one." We begin with the question of **genre**: we read the creation stories of Israel's neighbors, weigh the literary shape of Genesis 1, and notice the clues the author left in plain sight — a talking snake, a strange fixation on nakedness and shame — that tell us what *kind* of writing this is. Only once we know how to read it do we walk the text.

## How to use this site

Each session has its own page with the passage, background reading, discussion questions, and Hebrew/ANE notes. Work through them in order with your group, or jump to a session below. Leaders: skim the background notes before meeting; they flag the ANE parallels worth raising out loud.

**Part 1 — Learning to Read Genesis (the genre question)**

1. [The Neighbors' Stories](sessions/01-the-neighbors-stories/) — Ancient Near Eastern creation myths: *Enuma Elish*, *Atrahasis*, and the Memphite theology. What a "creation account" *was* in that world, and what Israel's first hearers already had ringing in their ears. 🎧 Podcasts: [short (19m)](sessions/01-the-neighbors-stories/Shorter_Podcast.m4a) · [long (40m)](sessions/01-the-neighbors-stories/Longer_Podcast.m4a)
2. [History or Poetry?](sessions/02-history-or-poetry/) — how the rabbis read Genesis 1 (the "ten utterances" of *Pirkei Avot* 5) and the literary *shape* of the text: forming (days 1–3) and filling (days 4–6), the hidden architecture of sevens, and the chiasm that turns on sacred time (*mōʿăḏîm*) and lands on Sabbath rest. Full text of Genesis 1:1–2:3 laid out block by block. 🎧 Podcasts: [medium (20m)](sessions/02-history-or-poetry/Medium_Podcast.m4a) · [long (40m)](sessions/02-history-or-poetry/Longer_Podcast.m4a)
3. [Clues in Plain Sight](sessions/03-clues-in-plain-sight/) — the "problems" the author left on purpose: a talking snake, the odd fixation on nakedness and shame, two creation accounts that don't line up. Twenty-eight of them, sorted by who spots each (child → philosopher) and by the job each one does — out-of-place signals that this was never meant to be read as flat, modern history. 🎧 Podcasts: [medium (19m)](sessions/03-clues-in-plain-sight/Medium_Podcast.m4a) · [long (40m)](sessions/03-clues-in-plain-sight/Longer_Podcast.m4a)

**Part 2 — Primeval History (Genesis 1–11)**

4. [In the Beginning](sessions/04-in-the-beginning/) — Genesis 1:1–2:3 re-read now that we know its genre. The seven Session 3 "problems" that live in this passage, walked one at a time — but *not* solved: each bump gets its standard fix named and declined, then followed as a signpost. All seven arrows land in the same place: **temple cosmology**. God's "rest" is a *move-in*, not a nap; the image is the statue installed in the finished house; the week is a sanctuary dedication. With the rabbis who pointed this way — *Numbers Rabbah* 12:13 ("the Tabernacle is equal to the world"), Hillel and Shammai arguing Genesis 1:1 with a throne and a palace, the Levites singing the creation week in the Temple — and the places they went elsewhere instead. Also: the [full reference version](sessions/04-in-the-beginning/reference.html) (three times as long, every source quoted) and source sheets for [the rabbis on Genesis 1](sessions/04-in-the-beginning/texts/rabbinic-sources.md) · [temple and cosmos](sessions/04-in-the-beginning/texts/temple-and-cosmos.md) 🎧 Podcasts: [medium (20m)](sessions/04-in-the-beginning/Medium_Podcast.m4a) · [long (49m)](sessions/04-in-the-beginning/Longer_Podcast.m4a)
5. [The Garden](sessions/05-the-garden/) — Genesis 2–3, read along one thread: **nakedness**, the word the story puts at its exact center. What "naked and not ashamed" claims (and why nakedness is a *vestment* problem once you notice Eden is a sanctuary); who the snake is — Genesis calls him a field animal God made, and the identification with Satan turns out to arrive centuries later; and how he works. His method is four moves, and the first one is to quote God's most generous sentence back with the polarity reversed: *from every tree you may freely eat* becomes *you must not eat from any tree*. The con is never "here is something evil" — it is **"look at what you're missing"** — and what it actually pays out is not the promotion but a mirror. Then the difference between guilt and shame, and God's sharpest question: *who told you that you were naked?* With the earliest readers on all of it, dated — Ben Sira (c. 180 BCE), Jubilees, Josephus, the Apocalypse of Moses — and the rabbis, including the fifth-century midrash that puts *"hurry and eat now, before…"* in the serpent's mouth. Also: the [full reference version](sessions/05-the-garden/reference.html) (every source quoted and dated, plus the material the class version leaves out) and source sheets for [the earliest readings](sessions/05-the-garden/texts/second-temple-sources.md) · [the rabbis on Genesis 3](sessions/05-the-garden/texts/rabbinic-sources.md) 🎧 Podcasts: [medium (22m)](sessions/05-the-garden/Medium_Podcast.m4a) · [long (30m)](sessions/05-the-garden/Longer_Podcast.m4a)

    **5.2 [The Garden, Part 2](sessions/05-the-garden/part-2.html)** — the second meeting on Genesis 2–3, picking up where the clock ran out. **Part 6** first — the practical landings the class never reached — then the **last seven of Session 3's twenty-eight problems**, braided into three threads. **Dust that can name:** *Genesis Rabbah* 17:4, where the angels cannot name the animals, Adam can, and he names himself from the *adamah* before naming God. **The tree you could refuse:** Ben Sira (c. 180 BCE) on the *yetzer* — a command that cannot be refused is not a command — and why the tree confers *jurisdiction*, not information. **Where the center is:** *Jubilees* 8:19 calling Eden the holy of holies, so the river paragraph is a survey marker, and the river returns in Ezekiel 47 and Revelation 22 with the tree of life unbarred. Closes with a map of all twenty-eight problems and the synthesis the whole arc has been building toward: **the creation accounts are not about creation — they are about the Creator, and His last and most costly creature.** His design, our rejection, and the plan. 🎧 Podcasts: [medium (23m)](sessions/05-the-garden/Part2_Medium_Podcast.m4a) · [long (32m)](sessions/05-the-garden/Part2_Longer_Podcast.m4a)

6. [Cain, Lamech, and the Spread of Violence](sessions/06-cain-and-violence/) — Genesis 4, read as **arithmetic**: something in the chapter keeps escalating and something keeps refusing to. Why the offering was refused — the text gives three asymmetries and no verdict, and the answer most of us were taught (he kept back the firstfruits) turns out to be Philo's argument from silence, c. 30 CE. Then the door: *ḥaṭṭāʾt* is feminine but *rōbēṣ* is **masculine**, and the verb is cognate with the Akkadian **rābiṣu**, the demon that waits at a threshold — while verse 7 quotes Genesis 3:16 word for word, so what broke the first marriage is now outdoors. Then **the mark** — *ʾôt* is the rainbow-and-Sabbath **covenant** word, the Hebrew says God set a sign ***for*** Cain, and its only stated job is to keep him alive; with the *nāśāʾ* thread that ties God's offered "lifting" in 4:7 to Cain's *"too great to be lifted"* in 4:13, and the midrash where Adam discovers the power of repentance from the first murderer — plus the dissent, in the same paragraph. Also: where Cain's wife came from, and what happens when you actually trace the names — they lead to the language of whichever book invented them, not to a people group. Then the genealogy read as **an answer to the curse**: the ground will no longer yield to Cain, and his line goes on to invent a city, tents, the harp and the flute, and bronze — **everything except farming**. The trade-names cluster exactly there (*Jabal* "to lead," *Jubal* / *yôḇēl* the ram's horn, *Tubal-Cain* holding *qayin*, "smith" — Cain's own name), and the city is Cain declining the sign he was already given and walling in his own protection instead, which is how Josephus read it. Lands on Lamech's seventy-seven and Matthew 18:22, which is the same number in the same Greek, run in the other direction. Source sheets: [Genesis 4 with the Hebrew notes](sessions/06-cain-and-violence/texts/genesis-4.html) · [the earliest readings](sessions/06-cain-and-violence/texts/second-temple-sources.html) · [the rabbis on Genesis 4](sessions/06-cain-and-violence/texts/rabbinic-sources.html) · [where the wives came from](sessions/06-cain-and-violence/texts/the-wives.html) 🎧 Podcasts: [medium (20m)](sessions/06-cain-and-violence/Medium_Podcast.m4a) · [long (55m)](sessions/06-cain-and-violence/Longer_Podcast.m4a)
7. [The Flood](sessions/07-the-flood/) — Genesis 6–9 against *Atrahasis* and *Gilgamesh*
8. [Babel](sessions/08-babel/) — Genesis 11 and the scattering of the nations
