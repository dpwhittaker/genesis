# The Flood, told twice: Genesis 6:5–9:19

The passage for **Session 9** (Part 4) and for next week's **Session 10**,
printed whole so you can work in it. It is coloured to show the two strands
that most critical scholars find woven together here — the one that calls God
***the LORD*** (scholars' "J") and the one that calls him ***God*** ("P"). The
division is the standard one, as set out by S. R. Driver (1913) and John
Skinner (1910); where later scholars draw a line differently, the notes below
say so.

**The colours are a map of one reading, not a verdict.** The handout lays out
three ways of reading these doublets — two strands woven together, one story
built as a mirror, and the tradition's one Author for whom nothing is said
twice for nothing — and you may hold any of them. Whichever you hold, the colours let you see exactly what each
reading is talking about.

**Three ways to read it.** First, read it straight through, as it stands in
our Bibles. Then press **the LORD (J) only** and read that strand by itself;
then **God (P) only**. Ask: does each strand read as a story on its own? What
is missing from each? Where do they disagree — and where does the whole say
something neither half says alone?

## Where scholars draw the lines differently

The division above is the one Skinner called *"generally accepted by recent
critics"* in 1910, and it is still the starting point. But the strand-makers
disagree at the edges, and the disagreements are worth knowing:

- **7:8–9** (dashed) — Driver: J, with *"two and two," "male and female"* and
  *"God"* added by the editor from P. Skinner: a later insertion. Friedman
  (2003): P.
- **7:17** — Driver and Skinner give *"The flood engulfed the earth"* to P and
  call *"for 40 days"* a J addition; Dillmann, Wellhausen and Friedman give the
  whole verse to J. Friedman also gives **7:18–20** to J.
- **8:3 and 8:13** — Wellhausen gives both verses entirely to P.
- **8:4** — Skinner thought the landing on Ararat might be J.
- **8:7**, the raven — Friedman gives it to P and the dove to J. (Some think
  the raven is a later addition.)
- **7:6** — Friedman (2003) assigns it to a separate list-source.

Two honest admissions from the critics themselves: **the J strand is not
complete** — it has no instructions for building the ark and no exit from it
(Driver, Skinner and Wellhausen all say so) — and **the P strand is nearly
whole**. Their explanation is that the editor kept P entire and used J to fill
it out.

## Ten notes on the Hebrew

### 1. "Ruined… ruined… ruined… destroy" (6:11–13)

One verb, ***šāḥat***, four times in three verses: *the earth was **ruined**…
it was **ruined**, for all flesh had **ruined** its way… I am about to
**ruin** them with the earth*. The NET varies its English (*ruined*,
*sinful*, *destroy*); the Hebrew repeats itself on purpose. The punishment is
the crime come home. The same verb returns in 6:17 and in the promise of 9:11
and 9:15 — *never again… to **ruin** the earth*.

### 2. "God saw… and indeed" (6:12 and 1:31)

> God saw all that he had made—and it was very good! — *1:31*

> God saw the earth, and indeed it was ruined… — *6:12*

The Hebrew is the same sentence frame both times — *wayyarʾ ʾĕlōhîm… wəhinnēh*,
"God saw… and look!" The same look; the opposite verdict.

### 3. Two verbs for the end

The LORD strand says ***māḥâ***, *wipe out* — the verb for wiping a dish
(6:7; 7:4; 7:23), and the verb of Moses' prayer at Sinai (Exodus 32:32). The
God strand says ***šāḥat***, *ruin, destroy* (note 1).

### 4. Pairs: "a man and his mate," "male and female"

7:2 says *seven pairs… **ʾîš wəʾištô***, literally *"a man and his wife"*, of
animals. Elsewhere the pairs are ***zākār ûnəqēbâ***, *"male and female"*
(6:19; 7:9; 7:16) — the phrase of Genesis 1:27, *"male and female he created
them."* The two words appear side by side in only seven verses of the Bible:
Genesis 1:27; 5:2; 6:19; 7:3; 7:9; 7:16; and Leviticus 15:33. (One small
crack in the neat division: 7:3, in the LORD strand, uses *male and female*
for the birds.)

### 5. The waters

The LORD strand has **rain** — *geshem*, 7:12 (and *I will cause it to rain*,
7:4). The God strand opens **the fountains of the great deep** and **the
floodgates of the heavens** (7:11; closed again in 8:2). *The deep* is
*təhôm* — the *watery deep* of Genesis 1:2; *floodgates*, *ʾărubbôt*, appear in
Genesis only here. In the God strand the Flood is creation running backward:
the waters God separated on Day 2 come together again. And *the flood* itself,
*mabbûl*, is a word the Bible uses only for this Flood — in Genesis 6–11 and
once in Psalm 29:10, *"The LORD sits enthroned over the engulfing waters."*

### 6. Both names in one verse (7:16)

> Those that entered were male and female, **just as God commanded him**.
> **Then the LORD shut him in.**

The source critics divide the verse in the middle. Cassuto heard in the second
half *"a father full of compassion towards his son"*; Skinner, from the other
side, called it *"a fine anthropomorphism."* Rashi reads the second half
without any comment on the name: *"He shut the door in front of him against
the waters."*

### 7. Two hearts, again (6:5–6 and 8:21)

The noun ***yēṣer***, *inclination*, appears in Genesis only at 6:5 and 8:21.
And *to his heart* frames the whole: *it pained him **to his heart*** (6:6);
*the LORD said **to his heart***, "I will never again…" (8:21, the NET's *said
to himself*).

### 8. "Remember"

*But God **remembered** Noah* (8:1) — the centre of Wenham's mirror. The verb
***zākar*** comes back only twice in the story, both times in God's mouth and
both times about the bow: *"I will **remember** my covenant… I will notice it
and **remember** the perpetual covenant"* (9:15–16).

### 9. The calendar

The God strand keeps a date-book, by the year of Noah's life. It never says
how long a month is, but it shows us: from the seventeenth day of the second
month (7:11) to the seventeenth day of the seventh (8:4) is five months, and the
text calls that span **150 days** (7:24; 8:3). Five months, 150 days — so its
months are **30 days** long. Counting that way, from the day the fountains
burst:

| Date (Noah's year / month / day) | Days in | Since the last date | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 600 / 2 / 17 | 0 | — | the fountains burst; Noah goes in (7:11, 13) |
| 600 / 7 / 17 | 150 | 150 days | the ark rests on the mountains of Ararat — the end of the "150 days" (7:24; 8:3–4) |
| 600 / 10 / 1 | 224 | 74 days | the mountain tops appear (8:5) |
| 601 / 1 / 1 | 314 | 90 days | the waters have dried up; the covering comes off (8:13) |
| 601 / 2 / 27 | 370 | 56 days | the earth is dry; God says, *"Come out"* (8:14–16) |
{: .ff-wrap}

**A year and ten days, door to door: 370 days.** Rashi counted it another way.
On a lunar calendar (twelve months of 29 and 30 days, 354 days in all), the
same dates come to about a solar year, and he took that to be the point: *"the
ten days are those by which the solar year exceeds the lunar year; for the
punishment of the generation of the Flood lasted a full (i.e. a solar) year"*
(Rashi on 8:14, from *Genesis Rabbah*). Either way, Noah is in the ark for about
a year.

**Which season?** The text doesn't say which month is "first." The rabbis
disagreed: *"Rabbi Eliezer said, 'This is the month Marcheshvan'; Rabbi Joshua
said, 'This is the month Eyar'"* (Rashi on 7:11, from *Rosh Hashanah* 11b). On
Rabbi Eliezer's count the year starts in the autumn, so the flood begins in
October–November, when the rains come. On Rabbi Joshua's it starts in the
spring (as Exodus 12:2 later starts Israel's year), so the flood begins in
April–May.

The LORD strand counts instead in sevens and forties: 7 days (7:4, 10), 40
days and 40 nights (7:4, 12), 40 days (8:6), 7 more days, 7 more days (8:10,
12). Laid end to end, that is about 101 days, a little over three months. Read
with the dates, the forty days of rain fall inside the first 150.

### 10. "Never again" — twice, and twice

*"I will **never again** curse the ground… I will **never again** destroy
everything that lives"* (8:21) — the LORD, to his heart. Then, from God, to
Noah: *"**Never again** will all living things be wiped out… **never again**
will a flood destroy the earth"* (9:11), and again in 9:15. Rashi on 8:21:
*"He repeated the expression that it might serve as a solemn oath."*
