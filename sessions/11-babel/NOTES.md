## Threads Session 10 hands forward (2026-10-15)

Session 10 (*The Flood Everyone Knew*) ended its "Next week" on Genesis
10–11: *"Noah's three sons become the nations of the world — and the nations
build a city and a tower, 'so that we may make a name for ourselves' (11:4).
Gilgamesh wanted a name too."* So this session is expected to take **Genesis
9:18–10:32 as well as 11:1–9** — or to split, per the chapter-a-week pacing.

- **Ham and Canaan (9:18–29).** Session 10 gave only one careful paragraph
  (divergence 7 and a "note on 9:25"): the curse falls on *Canaan*, not Ham;
  nothing about skin or Africa; *Genesis Rabbah* 36:7 ("Ḥam committed the sin
  and Canaan was cursed? This is astonishing") and Justin, *Dialogue* 139,
  answer that God had already blessed Noah's sons; the "curse of Ham" and
  slavery (Goldenberg 2003; Haynes 2002); the SBC's 2018 renunciation;
  Augustine, *City of God* 19.15, on the first "slave." The fuller material is
  on `../10-the-flood/texts/early-readers.md` §8, and in the Session 10
  research report (*Sanhedrin* 70a — Rav and Shmuel on what Ham did; the
  "darkened" folk-tales in *Sanhedrin* 108b and GR 36:7, where "turned black"
  is Steinsaltz's gloss, not the Talmud's words — handle with great care, if
  at all). Genesis 10 lists Ham's sons (Cush, Mizraim, Put, Canaan), which is
  where the misuse attached itself; the session should say plainly what the
  Table of Nations does and does not claim.
- **"Began" (*wayyāḥel*)** marks new starts that go wrong: 6:1; 9:20; 10:8
  (Nimrod "began to be a mighty one"); 11:6 (Babel, "this is what they have
  begun to do").
- **A name.** Gilgamesh "wanted a name" (Session 8); the men of the name
  (6:4); Babel's "let us make a name for ourselves" (11:4); and God's "I will
  make your name great" to Abram (12:2).
- **Shem's line** (11:10–26) runs ten generations to Abram, as Seth's ran ten
  to Noah (Session 7); *Pirkei Avot* 5:2 counts both sets of ten.
- **eBL's corpus English is not openly licensed** (see
  `../10-the-flood/NOTES.md`). For any Mesopotamian text (e.g. *Enmerkar and
  the Lord of Aratta*'s confusion of tongues, ETCSL 1.8.2.3, CC BY-NC-SA),
  prefer ETCSL or pre-1929 translations.
