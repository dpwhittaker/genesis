## Research and verification (2026-10-15)

Four parallel agents (Mesopotamia; Genesis and its readers; world floods —
Africa/Asia/Europe; world floods — Americas/Australia/Pacific), each quoting
only from files it downloaded and script-checking every quotation (357 + 179 +
110 + 72). A final script re-checked all four sheets and the handout against
those downloads: every quotation is found in its source except our own labelled
working translations. Things that are easy to get wrong:

- **eBL's English is not cleared.** The CC BY-NC-SA sentence on ebl.lmu.de sits
  under About → *Library* (the fragment collection); the *Corpus* pages carry no
  licence, name B. R. Foster and A. R. George as translators, and the Impressum
  says "Alle Rechte vorbehalten." So Gilgamesh XI is quoted from **Clay 1922**
  (PD; literal) and **Thompson 1928** (PD in the US since 2024 and in the UK).
  *Session 1's* CC BY-NC-SA label on the eBL Akkadian rests on the same
  sentence — worth asking Enrique Jiménez (LMU) if certainty matters.
- **Clay misreads several lines:** XI 19 (misses Ea's *oath*); XI 166–167 (the
  necklace — Session 9's teaser quoted Clay's "I shall not forget my
  necklace," which modern editions read differently; the Session 10 handout
  says so and quotes Thompson); XI 199 (Ea vs Enlil, a manuscript variant);
  OB Atrahasis II i 3–4, 7 ("satiated," "God was absent," "those observing").
  Use Thompson for XI 19, 166–167, 185–187, 199.
- **Line numbers** are George's (2003/eBL 2022). Clay's equal George's to 132
  and are one lower from 133 on. XI 128 is "six days and six [or seven]
  nights" by manuscript; XI 141 "twelve" vs "fourteen."
- **No licensed English exists for Atrahasis OB Tablet III** (the hungry gods,
  the flies, the lament, the post-flood order). Our working translations are
  made from the transliteration in **N. Wasserman, *The Flood: The Akkadian
  Sources* (OBO 290, 2020)**, whose licence is contradictory (OAPEN/DOAB: CC
  BY-NC-ND 4.0; the book's imprint: all rights reserved). Only short lines are
  used, labelled. **Ask David** whether he is content with that, or wants
  paraphrase only.
- **The repo's eBL "SB Tablet III supplement" was not the flood** — it is the
  strike and the creation of humankind. The note in
  `../01-the-neighbors-stories/texts/_atrahasis-akkadian-source.md` was
  corrected 2026-10-15. OB II–III are still not on eBL (manuscript lists only).
- **Megiddo's Gilgamesh fragment** is Tablet VII material (Enkidu's death), not
  the flood. Hence the careful wording "the story was in the air of their
  world"; no flood tablet comes from Iron Age Israel.
- **Heidel 1946/1949 is in copyright** (renewed 1973, 1976). Described only.
- **Frymer-Kensky 1977 verified**: overpopulation is the problem, noise the
  symptom; *ḥāmās* as bloodshed polluting the earth; the Gen 9 laws as "the
  structural equivalent" of Enki's population controls. The overpopulation
  reading is contested (Oden: encroachment on the gods' sphere).
- **The hero chosen for piety is partly shared**: Ziusudra is "humble…
  reverent" (ETCSL); Berossus names piety; only Gilgamesh XI is silent. King
  1918 wrongly says Berossus gives no reason.
- **Who objects:** Ea's rebuke ("On the sinner place his sin") is a real moral
  objection; Wasserman compares Ezekiel 14. The handout says so plainly and
  answers with Gen 18:25.
- **Noah never speaks** in 6:9–9:24 (every speech verb has God as subject); his
  first words are 9:25–27. The "herald" is 2 Peter 2:5 and the tradition
  (GR 30:7 — the cedars are there, not in *Sanhedrin* 108; *Sanhedrin* 108a–b;
  Josephus; the Sibyl; 1 Clement; Theophilus). Terry 1899: "The O. T.
  narrative of the flood records nothing of Noah's preaching."
- ***kōper*/*kipper*** are homonyms (BDB root II vs I); the verb in 6:14 is the
  only Qal of the root. Driver 1904: "it is possible therefore that kōpher came
  into Heb., with the story, from Babylonia." Don't preach it as "atonement."
- **The war-bow reading**: Driver and Skinner (PD) give both sides; Westermann,
  Wenham and Sarna were *not* checked, so they are not named. "Pointed away
  from the earth" is popular embellishment and was cut.
- **World floods.** Africa and Egypt are the honest exceptions (Frazer 1918:
  "conspicuously absent"). The Genesis-like details (pairs, dove, rainbow,
  eight survivors) cluster in records made after missionary contact — Hawaiʻi
  1823 → 1878 is the cleanest case; Fiji's "eight"; Maasai/Merker 1904; the
  Welsh Dwyfan and Dwyfach are Iolo Morganwg's forgery. The Nahua "brew no more
  wine" is an old misreading (Velázquez). Beckwith 1940 is PD in the US only
  (not renewed); quoted for one sentence. Nunn & Reid 2016 is closed access,
  described only. Hopi was left out (Waters 1963 is copyrighted and disputed).
- **NET wording**: 6:3 is "They will remain for 120 more years" — don't quote
  "his days will be 120 years" as NET.

## Threads Session 7 hands forward

- **Noah as the answer to the cursed ground.** Session 7 ends on 5:29 — nine
  generations under the curse of 3:17 / 4:11–12, and Lamech naming his son
  *Rest*. Genesis 9:20, *"Noah, a man of the soil, began to plant a
  vineyard,"* is the payoff: the ground yields again after the flood. Session
  7 promised the class this lands here. *Genesis Rabbah* 25:2 (on the Session
  7 rabbinic sheet) has the ox and the furrow obeying the plough again, and
  the two other *n-w-ḥ* landings — the ark *rests* (8:4), the *restful*
  aroma (8:21).
- **Methuselah dies in the flood year** (MT arithmetic: 187 + 182 + 600 =
  969). Session 7 made the point; don't repeat it, but it is available.
- **Noah "walked with God"** (6:9) — only he and Enoch get the phrase. Session
  7's passage sheet, note 4, has the grammar (hithpael, *ʾet* not *lipnê*).
- **Naamah = Noah's wife** (*Genesis Rabbah* 23:3) — the two genealogies
  married. Already quoted on the Session 7 rabbinic sheet.
- **Genesis 6:3, "120 years"** — Session 7 read it as the cliff in the
  lifespan curve (the King List's post-flood collapse). *Genesis Rabbah* 26:2
  reads it as the age of liability, which is why Noah fathers at 500.
- **The Watchers / sons of God (6:1–4)** — now **Session 8's** whole subject
  (Genesis 6:1–8). See [`../08-sons-of-god/NOTES.md`](../08-sons-of-god/NOTES.md)
  for what it handed forward: the second telling of the flood's cause in
  6:9–13, the LORD's heart at 6:6 and 8:21, Og on the ark, and 1 Peter 3:19–20.
- **Sippar / the buried tablets.** Berossus has Xisuthrus bury "the entire
  book" at Sippar before the flood so that knowledge survives; Josephus gives
  Seth's line the same motif (pillars of brick and stone, *Ant.* 1.2.3). Both
  are on the Session 7 sheets if the flood session wants the "learning
  preserved through the flood" theme.
