# The sons of God and the giants, across the Bible

A source sheet for **Session 8**. Genesis 6:1–4 is four verses long, and it
leaves two threads hanging: **who the "sons of God" are**, and **what became
of the Nephilim**. Both threads run on through the rest of Scripture. This
sheet follows them, in the NET, with a line or two on each.

Part A is the heavenly court. Part B is the giants. Part C is the New
Testament, which picks up both.

---

## Part A — The heavenly court

The Old Testament regularly pictures God the way its world pictured a king:
**on a throne, with a court standing around him.** The court has several
names — the *heavenly assembly* or *host* (literally *army*), the *holy
ones*, the *council*, and the *sons of God*. God needs no advisers, and the
Bible never suggests he does. But he rules as a king rules, and a king has a
court.

### 1. The court in session

> Micaiah said, "That being the case, listen to the LORD's message. **I saw
> the LORD sitting on his throne, with all the heavenly assembly standing
> beside him on his right and on his left.** The LORD said, 'Who will deceive
> Ahab, so he will attack Ramoth Gilead and die there?' One said this and
> another that. Then a spirit stepped forward and stood before the LORD. He
> said, 'I will deceive him.'" — *1 Kings 22:19–21*

> Now the day came when **the sons of God came to present themselves before
> the LORD**—and Satan also arrived among them. — *Job 1:6* (again at 2:1)

> I heard the voice of the LORD say, "Whom will I send? **Who will go on our
> behalf?**" — *Isaiah 6:8*

> While I was watching, **thrones were set up**, and the Ancient of Days took
> his seat… Many thousands were ministering to him; many tens of thousands
> stood ready to serve him. **The court convened**, and the books were
> opened. — *Daniel 7:9–10*

> Yet which of them has ever **stood in the LORD's inner circle** so they
> could see and hear what he has to say? — *Jeremiah 23:18* (the Hebrew is
> *sôd*, "council": a true prophet has stood in God's council)

Job 1:6 is the only other place in the Bible with the exact phrase of Genesis
6:2, ***bənê hāʾĕlōhîm***, and there it is plainly the court of heaven.

### 2. The sons of God at creation, and in worship

> …when the **morning stars** sang in chorus, and **all the sons of God**
> shouted for joy? — *Job 38:7*

The two halves of the line say the same thing twice, as Hebrew poetry does:
*morning stars* = *sons of God*. This is why the court is also called the
**host of heaven** — the stars, pictured as a living army.

> Acknowledge the LORD, **you heavenly beings** [*bənê ʾēlîm*, "sons of
> gods"], acknowledge the LORD's majesty and power. — *Psalm 29:1*

> For who in the skies can compare to the LORD? Who is like the LORD **among
> the heavenly beings** [*bənê ʾēlîm*], a God who is honored **in the great
> angelic assembly**, and more awesome than all who surround him? — *Psalm
> 89:6–7*

### 3. Created, and worshipping

The court is not a rival pantheon. It is made, and it bows.

> You alone are the LORD. You made the heavens, even the highest heavens,
> along with all their multitude of stars… You impart life to them all, and
> **the multitudes of heaven worship you**. — *Nehemiah 9:6*

> Praise him, all his angels. Praise him, **all his heavenly assembly**…
> Let them praise the name of the LORD, **for he gave the command and they
> came into existence**. — *Psalm 148:2, 5*

### 4. The court judged

Members of the court can fail — and be judged.

> **God stands in the assembly of El; in the midst of the gods he renders
> judgment.** He says, "How long will you make unjust legal decisions and
> show favoritism to the wicked?… I thought, 'You are gods; all of you are
> **sons of the Most High**.' Yet you will die like mortals; you will fall
> like all the other rulers." — *Psalm 82:1–2, 6–7*

Jesus quotes verse 6 in John 10:34–36. Readers have long disagreed over
whether Psalm 82's "gods" are heavenly beings or human judges; the first line
sets the scene in *the assembly of El*, and the sentence — *you will die like
mortals* — is a strange one to pass on men who were mortal already.

### 5. The nations and their princes

> When the Most High gave the nations their inheritance, when he divided up
> humankind, he set the boundaries of the peoples, **according to the number
> of the heavenly assembly**. For the LORD's allotment is his people, Jacob
> is his special possession. — *Deuteronomy 32:8–9*

The NET is following a Dead Sea Scroll here (4QDeut<sup>j</sup>, "sons of
God"); the traditional Hebrew has "sons of Israel," the Greek Bible "angels
of God." Read the scroll's way, the verse says the nations were **assigned
to members of the heavenly court**, while the LORD kept Israel for himself.
Deuteronomy says something similar about the stars:

> When you look up to the sky and see the sun, moon, and stars—**the whole
> heavenly creation**—you must not be seduced to worship and serve them, for
> **the LORD your God has assigned them to all the people of the world**.
> — *Deuteronomy 4:19*

And Daniel's angel speaks of heavenly **princes** over Persia and Greece,
with Michael as Israel's:

> However, **the prince of the kingdom of Persia** was opposing me for 21
> days. But **Michael, one of the leading princes**, came to help me…
> — *Daniel 10:13* (also 10:20–21)

### 6. The "us" of Genesis

> Then God said, "**Let us make** humankind in **our** image, after **our**
> likeness…" — *Genesis 1:26*

> And the LORD God said, "Now that the man has become **like one of us**,
> knowing good and evil…" — *Genesis 3:22*

> "Come, **let's go down** and confuse their language…" — *Genesis 11:7*

Three times Genesis has God speak in the plural. Readers have heard three
things: God addressing **his court** (the rabbis — the Midrash has him
consult the angels before making Adam), the **Trinity** (the church fathers),
or a plural of **majesty** or of self-deliberation. Isaiah 6:8 — *"Who will go
for **us**?"*, spoken in the throne room with the seraphim present — is the
nearest parallel inside the Bible. Whichever you hold, notice where the
three verses fall: making humankind, humankind reaching to be *like one of
us*, and humankind building a tower *into the heavens*. The "us" speaks at
the border between heaven and earth.

### 7. A fall from the sky

> Look how you have **fallen from the sky, O shining one, son of the
> dawn!**… You said to yourself, "I will climb up to the sky. **Above the
> stars of El** I will set up my throne. I will rule on **the mountain of
> assembly** on the remote slopes of Zaphon… I will make myself like the
> Most High!" But you were brought down to Sheol. — *Isaiah 14:12–15*

The poem is aimed at the **king of Babylon** (14:4). But it taunts him in the
language of a heavenly rebel who tried to climb the mountain where the gods
assemble, and was thrown down — which is why the church has long heard in it
the fall of Satan. (Zaphon, "the north," is the name of the Canaanite gods'
mountain.)

> At that time the LORD will punish **the heavenly forces in the heavens**
> and the earthly kings on the earth. **They will be imprisoned in a pit,
> locked up in a prison**, and after staying there for a long time, they will
> be punished. — *Isaiah 24:21–22*

Heavenly powers, imprisoned until a later judgment. Hold that for Part C.

### 8. "Like a son of the gods" — and "his angel"

> "But I see four men, untied and walking around in the midst of the fire!
> No harm has come to them! And **the appearance of the fourth is like that
> of a god!**" — *Daniel 3:25*

> "Praised be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, **who has sent
> forth his angel** and has rescued his servants…" — *Daniel 3:28*

The Aramaic of 3:25 is literally ***"like a son of the gods"*** (*bar
ʾĕlāhîn*). Three verses later the same speaker calls the same figure **God's
angel**. Inside one chapter, a "son of the gods" and an "angel" are the same
thing.

### 9. The Watchers

> While I was watching in my mind's visions on my bed, **a holy sentinel came
> down from heaven**. — *Daniel 4:13*

> This announcement is by **the decree of the sentinels**; this decision is by
> the pronouncement of **the holy ones**… — *Daniel 4:17*

The NET's *sentinel* is the Aramaic ***ʿîr***, "**one who is awake**" — a
**Watcher**. Daniel 4 is the only chapter in the Bible that uses the word. In
Jewish writings of the same centuries it became the name for the angels of
Genesis 6: the Watchers who *came down*. See [the earliest
readings](second-temple-sources.html).

## Part B — The giants

### 1. The Nephilim, once more — in a report the Bible calls evil

> Then they presented the Israelites with **a discouraging report** of the
> land they had investigated, saying, "…All the people we saw there are of
> great stature. **We even saw the Nephilim there** (the descendants of Anak
> came from the Nephilim), and **we seemed like grasshoppers** both to
> ourselves and to them." — *Numbers 13:32–33*

> The men whom Moses sent to investigate the land, who returned and made the
> whole community murmur against him by producing **an evil report** about
> the land, those men who produced the evil report about the land, **died by
> the plague before the LORD**. — *Numbers 14:36–37*

This is the only place after the flood where the Nephilim appear by name —
and it is in the mouth of ten men whose report the text calls *dibbâ*,
"slander, evil report." The parenthesis linking the Anakim to the Nephilim is
missing from the Greek Bible. And the spies' last clause — *we seemed like
grasshoppers **to them*** — turns out to be wrong. Forty years later, a woman
in Jericho tells two other spies what the Canaanites actually felt:

> "…**We are absolutely terrified of you**, and all who live in the land are
> cringing before you. For we heard how the LORD… annihilated the two Amorite
> kings, **Sihon and Og**… When we heard the news, we lost our courage."
> — *Joshua 2:9–11*

### 2. The Anakim

> When they went up through the Negev, they came to **Hebron** where Ahiman,
> Sheshai, and Talmai, **descendants of Anak**, were living. — *Numbers
> 13:22*

> They include the Anakites, **a numerous and tall people** whom you know
> about and of whom it is said, "Who is able to resist the Anakites?"
> — *Deuteronomy 9:2*

> At that time Joshua attacked and eliminated the Anakites from the hill
> country… No Anakites were left in Israelite territory, **though some
> remained in Gaza, Gath, and Ashdod**. — *Joshua 11:21–22*

And the one spy who did not bring the evil report asks for the giants' own
city, at eighty-five:

> "Today I am still as strong as when Moses sent me out… Now, assign me this
> hill country that the LORD promised me at that time! No doubt you heard then
> that **the Anakites live there in large, fortified cities**. But assuming
> the LORD is with me, I will conquer them." …(Hebron used to be called
> Kiriath Arba. **Arba was a famous Anakite.**) — *Joshua 14:11–12, 15*

### 3. The Rephaim, and the names other peoples gave them

> (The Emites used to live there, a people as powerful, numerous, and **tall
> as the Anakites**. These people, as well as the Anakites, are also
> considered **Rephaites**; the Moabites call them Emites.) — *Deuteronomy
> 2:10–11*

> (That also is considered to be a land of the Rephaites. The Rephaites lived
> there originally; **the Ammonites call them Zamzummites**. They are a people
> as powerful, numerous, and tall as the Anakites.) — *Deuteronomy 2:20–21*

> **Only King Og of Bashan was left of the remaining Rephaites.** (It is
> noteworthy that his sarcophagus was made of iron. Does it not, indeed, still
> remain in Rabbath of the Ammonites? It is 13½ feet long and 6 feet wide
> according to standard measure.) — *Deuteronomy 3:11*

> The territory of King Og of Bashan, one of the few remaining Rephaites,
> **who lived in Ashtaroth and Edrei and ruled over Mount Hermon**…
> — *Joshua 12:4–5*

> For Israel's sake I destroyed the Amorites. **They were as tall as cedars
> and as strong as oaks.** — *Amos 2:9*

Og's "sarcophagus" is literally his ***bed*** (*ʿereś*), "a bed of iron," nine
cubits by four. Notice the three details Joshua gives him: the cities of
**Ashtaroth and Edrei**, and **Mount Hermon**. All three matter on [the
neighbours sheet](the-neighbors.html).

### 4. Gath, and Goliath

> Then a champion came out from the camp of the Philistines. His name was
> **Goliath; he was from Gath**. He was **close to seven feet tall**.
> — *1 Samuel 17:4*

Your Bible may say something different here, and both are honest. The
traditional Hebrew text says Goliath was **"six cubits and a span"** — about
nine and a half feet, the figure in the KJV, NIV and ESV. A Dead Sea Scroll
copy of Samuel (4QSam<sup>a</sup>), the Greek Bible, and Josephus all say
**"four cubits and a span"** — about six foot nine. The NET follows the older
copies. Either way, he was a giant to the men facing him.

> Yet another battle occurred in Gath. On that occasion there was **a large
> man who had six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot**, twenty-four
> in all! **He too was a descendant of Rapha**… These four were the
> descendants of Rapha who lived in Gath; **they were killed by David and his
> soldiers**. — *2 Samuel 21:20, 22* (also 1 Chronicles 20:4–8, which says
> *Rephaim*)

**Follow the thread and it ends here.** Nephilim → Anakim (Numbers 13:33) →
reckoned among the Rephaim (Deuteronomy 2:11) → the last Anakim left in Gath
(Joshua 11:22) → Goliath of Gath and the four "descendants of Rapha" — killed
by David and his men. After David, the giants disappear from the Bible's
story.

### 5. The Rephaim as the dead

The same Hebrew word, ***rəpāʾîm***, has a second meaning. It is also the
Bible's word for **the dead in Sheol** — the "shades."

> Sheol below is stirred up about you, ready to meet you when you arrive. It
> rouses **the spirits of the dead** [*rəpāʾîm*] for you, all the former
> leaders of the earth; it makes all the former kings of the nations rise from
> their thrones. — *Isaiah 14:9*

> The dead do not come back to life, **the spirits of the dead** [*rəpāʾîm*]
> do not rise. — *Isaiah 26:14*

> **The dead** [*hārəpāʾîm*] tremble— those beneath the waters and all that
> live in them. — *Job 26:5*

Also Isaiah 26:19; Psalm 88:10; Proverbs 9:18. A race of ancient giant kings,
and the ghosts of dead kings, share a name. The [neighbours
sheet](the-neighbors.html) shows why: in Canaanite texts, the *rpum* are the
**royal dead**.

### 6. The fallen warriors of old

> They do not lie with **the fallen warriors of ancient times**, who went down
> to Sheol with their weapons of war, having their swords placed under their
> heads and their shields on their bones, when the terror of these warriors
> was in the land of the living. — *Ezekiel 32:27*

The Hebrew is *gibbôrîm nōpəlîm* — "**mighty men, fallen**" — the two words
of Genesis 6:4, side by side. (The traditional text says they fell "from the
uncircumcised," *mēʿărēlîm*; the Greek read *mēʿôlām*, "**of old**," exactly
as in Genesis 6:4, and the NET follows it.) Ezekiel's Sheol has a place for
the ancient heroes, buried with their swords.

## Part C — The New Testament

### 1. The angels who sinned, in the days of Noah

> …by being put to death in the flesh but by being made alive in the spirit.
> In it he went and preached to **the spirits in prison**, after they were
> disobedient long ago **when God patiently waited in the days of Noah** as an
> ark was being constructed. — *1 Peter 3:18–20*

> For if God did not spare **the angels who sinned**, but **threw them into
> hell** and locked them up **in chains in utter darkness**, to be kept until
> the judgment, and if he did not spare the ancient world, but did protect
> **Noah**, a herald of righteousness, along with seven others, when God
> brought **a flood** on an ungodly world, and if he turned to ashes the cities
> of **Sodom and Gomorrah**… — *2 Peter 2:4–6*

> You also know that **the angels who did not keep within their proper domain
> but abandoned their own place of residence**, he has kept in eternal chains
> in utter darkness, locked up for the judgment of the great Day. So also
> Sodom and Gomorrah and the neighboring towns, since they indulged in sexual
> immorality and **pursued unnatural desire in a way similar to these
> angels**… — *Jude 6–7*

Three letters, and one story. Angels **left their place**; they are **kept in
chains in darkness until the judgment**; it is tied to **the days of Noah**;
and it is set beside **Sodom**, whose sin was going after *"other flesh"*
(Jude 7, literally), *"in a way similar to these angels."* Two details:
the NET's *threw them into hell* in 2 Peter is one Greek verb,
***tartarōsas***, "**consigned them to Tartarus**" — the Greek name for the
pit where the Titans who fought the gods were chained. And Jude, a few lines
later, **quotes the Book of Enoch**:

> Now Enoch, the seventh in descent beginning with Adam, even prophesied of
> them, saying, "Look! The Lord is coming with thousands and thousands of his
> holy ones…" — *Jude 14* (= *1 Enoch* 1:9)

Jude knew the Enoch literature, where the Genesis 6 story is told at length —
angels who left heaven, were chained in darkness, and are held for the
judgment. See [the earliest readings](second-temple-sources.html), and the
session handout, Part 2, for how the church has read these verses.

### 2. "Like angels in heaven"

> "For in the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but
> **are like angels in heaven**." — *Matthew 22:30*

> "…they can no longer die because they are **equal to angels and are sons
> of God**, since they are sons of the resurrection." — *Luke 20:36*

This is the strongest verse for readers who hold that the sons of God in
Genesis 6 were **not** angels: angels do not marry. Those who read Genesis 6
as angels answer that Jesus says *angels in heaven* — the ones who kept
their place — and Jude's point is precisely that these did not. Notice also
Luke's phrasing: *equal to angels* and *sons of God*, side by side.

### 3. "As it was in the days of Noah"

> "For in those days before the flood, people were **eating and drinking,
> marrying and giving in marriage**, until the day Noah entered the ark. And
> **they knew nothing** until the flood came and took them all away. It will
> be the same at the coming of the Son of Man." — *Matthew 24:38–39* (also
> Luke 17:26–27)

When Jesus reaches for the days of Noah, he does not mention giants or
angels. He mentions **ordinary life, carried on without noticing**.

### 4. Other echoes, more debated

> For this reason a woman should have a symbol of authority on her head,
> **because of the angels**. — *1 Corinthians 11:10*

Tertullian (c. 200) connected this verse with Genesis 6; most modern readers
think Paul means the angels present at worship. It is a genuinely hard verse.

> They cried out, "Son of God, leave us alone! **Have you come here to
> torment us before the time?**" — *Matthew 8:29*
>
> And they began to beg him not to order them to depart **into the abyss**.
> — *Luke 8:31*

The demons know there is an appointed time and a place of confinement. Many
scholars hear the Enoch background — spirits held for a set judgment — behind
these lines; the Gospels themselves do not say where demons come from.

### 5. The sons of God, redefined

Genesis 6 is the last time in Genesis anyone is called a "son of God." When
the New Testament takes the phrase back, it turns it inside out.

> …the son of Enosh, the son of Seth, the son of Adam, **the son of God**.
> — *Luke 3:38*

> But when the appropriate time had come, **God sent out his Son, born of a
> woman**, born under the law, to redeem those who were under the law, **so
> that we may be adopted as sons** with full rights. — *Galatians 4:4–5*

> But to all who have received him—those who believe in his name—**he has
> given the right to become God's children**. — *John 1:12*

> For all who are led by the Spirit of God are **the sons of God**… For the
> creation eagerly waits for **the revelation of the sons of God**.
> — *Romans 8:14, 19*

> Therefore, since the children share in flesh and blood, **he likewise
> shared in their humanity**… For surely his concern is not for angels, but
> he is concerned for Abraham's descendants. — *Hebrews 2:14, 16*

In Genesis 6, beings from heaven come down and *take* what they want from
among the daughters of humankind, and their children are the heroes the world
remembers. In the Gospel, **the Son of God comes down, born of a woman, and
gives** — and the people he gives to become the sons of God.
