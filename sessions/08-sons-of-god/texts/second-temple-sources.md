# The earliest readings of Genesis 6:1–8

A source sheet for **Session 8**. Everything here was written **before the
rabbinic literature** — roughly **250 BCE to 100 CE** — which makes these the
oldest surviving readings of Genesis 6 in any language. For the rabbis, see
**[the rabbis on Genesis 6:1–8](rabbinic-sources.html)**; for what Israel's
neighbours said, **[the neighbours](the-neighbors.html)**.

**Why these matter here.** Genesis gives the sons of God four verses. By the
time of Jesus the story had a literature: the angels had names, a leader, a
mountain, an oath, a curriculum; their children had a height, a diet, and
dreams; and their ghosts were the demons. **Almost every Jewish reader of
this period read the sons of God as angels** — and the New Testament letters
of Jude and Peter were written by men who knew these books. Two things to
watch as you read: how fast a silence in Genesis becomes a story, and **where
each writer puts the blame**.

Each entry is marked **→ toward the session's reading** or **⇢ a different
direction**. The session reads the sons of God as heavenly beings (with Jude
and 2 Peter) *and* holds that Genesis puts the root of the problem in the
human heart (6:5). Several of these texts agree with the first and not the
second.

## 1. The Greek Bible — *c. 250 BCE* → *toward the session's reading*

The Jews of Alexandria translated the Torah into Greek in the third century
BCE. This is how their Genesis 6 reads, in the 1851 English of Sir Lancelot
Brenton:

> 1 And it came to pass when men began to be numerous upon the earth, and
> daughters were born to them, 2 that the sons of God having seen the
> daughters of men that they were beautiful, took to themselves wives of all
> whom they chose. 3 And the Lord God said, **My Spirit shall certainly not
> remain among these men for ever, because they are flesh**, but their days
> shall be an hundred and twenty years. 4 **Now the giants were upon the earth
> in those days**; and after that when the sons of God were wont to go in to
> the daughters of men, they bore children to them, **those were the giants of
> old, the men of renown.** — *Genesis 6:1–4 (Greek)*

Three things.

**"Giants" — twice.** The Greek uses *gigantes* for both the *nəpilîm* and
the *gibbōrîm* of verse 4. This is where "giants" enters the Bible. In
Greek, *gigantes* were the earth-born monsters who fought the gods of heaven
(see [the neighbours sheet](the-neighbors.html)) — so the translators were
naming a *kind* of being, not a height.

**"Angels of God."** One of the great early manuscripts, **Codex
Alexandrinus** (5th century), reads at 6:2 not "the sons of God" but ***"the
angels of God."*** So did the Greek Bibles of **Philo** and **Josephus** (see
below), and Cyril of Alexandria knew copies that *"clearly contain 'the
angels of God seeing the daughters of men.'"* The majority of manuscripts
read "sons of God," and most scholars think that is the original Greek. But
"angels" is how many Greek-speaking Jews first met this verse.

**The other giants.** The Greek of **Deuteronomy 32:8** says God set the
nations' bounds *"according to the number of the **angels** of God"*
(Brenton) — the Hebrew scroll found at Qumran (4QDeut<sup>j</sup>) says *"sons
of God"*, and the traditional Hebrew says *"sons of Israel."* The Greek of
**Numbers 13:33** has no parenthesis about the Anakim: *"And there we saw the
giants; and we were before them as locusts"* — the words *"the descendants of
Anak came from the Nephilim"* are not there. And the Greek of **Ezekiel
32:27** has the dead warriors lie *"with the **giants** that fell **of
old**"* — reading *mēʿôlām*, "of old," exactly as in Genesis 6:4.

### And a century later: the turn

In the second century CE three new Jewish translations into Greek were made,
and they show the turn to a human reading already under way:

| | 6:2 "the sons of God" | 6:4 "the Nephilim" |
|---|---|---|
| **Aquila** (c. 130 CE) | "the sons of **the gods**" | "**those who fall upon**" (attackers) |
| **Symmachus** (late 2nd c.) | "the sons of **the powerful**" | "**the violent ones**" |
| **Theodotion** (2nd c.) | "sons of God" | "giants" |
{: .ff-wrap}

Symmachus is the rabbis' reading in Greek. Aquila, a convert famous for
literalism, kept the plural *gods* — and Jerome, around 400,
explained that he meant *"holy ones or angels."* (Readings from Field's
*Hexapla*, 1875; English glosses ours.)

## 2. *1 Enoch*, the Book of the Watchers — *3rd century BCE* → *on the angels* · ⇢ *on the blame*

The oldest and most influential retelling. The "Book of the Watchers" is the
first part (chapters 1–36) of *1 Enoch*, written in Aramaic in the third
century BCE; fragments of it were found among the Dead Sea Scrolls. **Jude
14–15 quotes it.** The translation is R. H. Charles's (1917); we have dropped
his text-critical brackets.

### The descent

> 1. And it came to pass when the children of men had multiplied that in
> those days were born unto them beautiful and comely daughters. 2. **And the
> angels, the children of the heaven, saw and lusted after them**, and said to
> one another: 'Come, let us choose us wives from among the children of men
> and beget us children.' 3. And Semjâzâ, who was their leader, said unto them:
> 'I fear ye will not indeed agree to do this deed, and I alone shall have to
> pay the penalty of a great sin.' 4. And they all answered him and said: 'Let
> us all swear an oath, and all bind ourselves by mutual imprecations not to
> abandon this plan but to do this thing.' 5. Then sware they all together and
> bound themselves by mutual imprecations upon it. 6. **And they were in all
> two hundred; who descended in the days of Jared on the summit of Mount
> Hermon, and they called it Mount Hermon, because they had sworn and bound
> themselves by mutual imprecations upon it.** — *1 Enoch* 6:1–6

Two Hebrew puns, which Charles pointed out a century ago. They came down in
the days of **Jared** — *yered*, "descent" — because *yārad* means **to come
down**. And they swore on **Hermon** because *ḥērem* means **a curse, a
ban**. (Session 7's passage sheet met the Jared pun already.) Mount Hermon is
the great mountain at the head of the Jordan — the mountain Joshua 12:5 says
**Og** ruled.

### What they taught

> 1. And they became pregnant, and they bare great giants, **whose height was
> three thousand ells**: 3. Who consumed all the acquisitions of men. 4. And
> when men could no longer sustain them, the giants turned against them and
> devoured mankind… 6. **Then the earth laid accusation against the lawless
> ones.** — *1 Enoch* 7:2–6
>
> 1. **And Azâzêl taught men to make swords, and knives, and shields, and
> breastplates, and made known to them the metals of the earth and the art of
> working them, and bracelets, and ornaments, and the use of antimony, and the
> beautifying of the eyelids, and all kinds of costly stones, and all
> colouring tinctures.** 2. And there arose much godlessness, and they
> committed fornication, and they were led astray, and became corrupt in all
> their ways. 3. Semjâzâ taught enchantments, and root-cuttings, Armârôs the
> resolving of enchantments, Barâqîjâl (taught) astrology, Kôkabêl the
> constellations, Êzêqêêl the knowledge of the clouds… and Sariêl the course
> of the moon. — *1 Enoch* 8:1–3

An "ell" is a cubit; three thousand of them is nearly a mile. Nothing in
Genesis says the Nephilim were tall. By the third century BCE they were the
height of a mountain.

And look at the **curriculum**: metalwork and weapons, jewellery and
cosmetics, spells, the signs of the sky. Session 6 found Genesis crediting
metalwork to **Tubal-Cain**, and Session 7 found Babylon crediting the arts to
the ***apkallu***, sages from beyond. *1 Enoch* takes the same list and says it
was brought by rebel angels. What Babylon called a gift, *1 Enoch* calls
**stolen goods**.

### The verdict

> 4. And again the Lord said to Raphael: '**Bind Azâzêl hand and foot, and
> cast him into the darkness: and make an opening in the desert, which is in
> Dûdâêl, and cast him therein.** 5. And place upon him rough and jagged
> rocks, and cover him with darkness, and let him abide there for ever, and
> cover his face that he may not see light. 6. And on the day of the great
> judgement he shall be cast into the fire…' 9. And to Gabriel said the Lord:
> 'Proceed against the bastards and the reprobates… **send them one against
> the other that they may destroy each other in battle**…' 11. And the Lord
> said unto Michael: 'Go, bind Semjâzâ and his associates… 12. And when their
> sons have slain one another, and they have seen the destruction of their
> beloved ones, **bind them fast for seventy generations in the valleys of
> the earth, till the day of their judgement** and of their consummation…'
> — *1 Enoch* 10:4–6, 9, 11–12

Set that beside the New Testament: angels *"kept in eternal chains in utter
darkness, locked up for the judgment of the great Day"* (Jude 6); *"locked up
in chains in utter darkness, to be kept until the judgment"* (2 Peter 2:4).
Chains, darkness, held until the judgment — the pieces are the same.

And **Azazel**, bound in the **desert**: on the Day of Atonement, Israel sent
a goat *"into the desert to Azazel"* (Leviticus 16:10). The Talmud later
says Azazel is so called *"because it atones for the actions of Uzza and
Azael"* (see [the rabbinic sheet](rabbinic-sources.html)).

### Where demons come from

Enoch is sent to tell the Watchers that their plea for mercy is refused:

> 3. Wherefore have ye left the high, holy, and eternal heaven, and lain with
> women, and defiled yourselves with the daughters of men and taken to
> yourselves wives, and done like the children of earth, and begotten giants
> (as your) sons?… 6. But you were formerly spiritual, living the eternal
> life, and immortal for all generations of the world. 7. And therefore I have
> not appointed wives for you; **for as for the spiritual ones of the heaven,
> in heaven is their dwelling.** 8. **And now, the giants, who are produced
> from the spirits and flesh, shall be called evil spirits upon the earth, and
> on the earth shall be their dwelling.**… 11. And the spirits of the giants
> afflict, oppress, destroy, attack, do battle, and work destruction on the
> earth… — *1 Enoch* 15:3, 6–8, 11

This is the answer many Jews of Jesus' day would have given to *where do
demons come from?* — **the ghosts of the giants.** Notice also verse 7:
*"in heaven is their dwelling."* It is almost Jesus's phrase, *"like angels
**in heaven**"* (Matthew 22:30) — and *1 Enoch*'s point is that these angels
*left* heaven.

### Where *1 Enoch* puts the blame

> 8. And the whole earth has been corrupted through the works that were
> taught by Azâzêl: **to him ascribe all sin.** — *1 Enoch* 10:8

That is the sentence Genesis will not write. In *1 Enoch*, evil came *down*
on humanity. In Genesis, the LORD looks at *humankind* and sees that *every
inclination of the thoughts of their hearts was only evil* (6:5). ⇢

## 3. *Jubilees* — *c. 160–150 BCE* → *on the angels* · *and a different job for them*

A Hebrew retelling of Genesis and Exodus, found at Qumran and surviving whole
only in Ethiopic. English: R. H. Charles (1917).

First, where Session 7 left the Watchers — in Jared's day, and **sent to
help**:

> …and he called his name Jared; **for in his days the angels of the Lord
> descended on the earth, those who are named the Watchers, that they should
> instruct the children of men, and that they should do judgment and
> uprightness on the earth.** — *Jubilees* 4:15

In *Jubilees* the Watchers came down on an errand from God — and fell once
they arrived. Then Genesis 6, retold:

> V. And it came to pass when the children of men began to multiply on the
> face of the earth and daughters were born unto them, that **the angels of
> God** saw them on a certain year of this jubilee, that they were beautiful
> to look upon; and they took themselves wives of all whom they chose, and they
> bare unto them sons and they were giants. 2. And lawlessness increased on
> the earth and all flesh corrupted its way, alike men and cattle and beasts
> and birds and everything that walketh on the earth… **and every imagination
> of the thoughts of all men (was) thus evil continually.** 3. And God looked
> upon the earth, and behold it was corrupt… 5. But Noah found grace before
> the eyes of the Lord. 6. And against the angels whom He had sent upon the
> earth, He was exceedingly wroth, and He gave commandment to root them out of
> all their dominion, and **He bade us to bind them in the depths of the
> earth**, and behold they are bound in the midst of them, and are (kept)
> separate. 7. And against their sons went forth a command from before His
> face that they should be smitten with the sword, and be removed from under
> heaven. 8. And He said "[My] spirit will not always abide on man; for they
> also are flesh and their days shall be one hundred and twenty years." 9. And
> He sent His sword into their midst that each should slay his neighbour, and
> they began to slay each other till they all fell by the sword and were
> destroyed from the earth. — *Jubilees* 5:1–3, 5–9

*Jubilees* does something *1 Enoch* does not: it keeps **Genesis 6:5** —
*every imagination of the thoughts of all men… evil continually.* The angels
fell, *and* the human heart was evil. (The "us" who bind the angels are the
angels of God's presence, who narrate the book. In verse 8 the 1917 printing
has *"Thy spirit,"* a misprint for *My*.)

Noah, later, tells his grandsons why the flood came, and gives the Nephilim
their fullest family tree:

> 21. For owing to these three things came the flood upon the earth, namely,
> owing to the fornication wherein **the Watchers against the law of their
> ordinances went a whoring after the daughters of men**, and took themselves
> wives of all which they chose: and they made the beginning of uncleanness.
> 22. And they begat sons the Nâphîdîm, and they were all unlike, and they
> devoured one another: **and the Giants slew the Nâphîl, and the Nâphîl slew
> the Eljô, and the Eljô mankind, and one man another.** — *Jubilees*
> 7:21–22

Three generations of giants, each destroying the next — the same three kinds
a Greek copy of *1 Enoch* 7:2 lists (*"first great giants, and the giants
begat the Naphilim, and to the Naphilim were born the Eliûd"*).

And after the flood, the spirits:

> 8. And the chief of the spirits, Mastêmâ, came and said: "Lord, Creator, let
> some of them remain before me… for if some of them are not left to me, I
> shall not be able to execute the power of my will on the sons of men…" 9.
> And He said: "**Let the tenth part of them remain before him, and let nine
> parts descend into the place of condemnation.**"… 12. And we explained to
> Noah all the medicines of their diseases, together with their seductions,
> how he might heal them with herbs of the earth. 13. **And Noah wrote down all
> things in a book** as we instructed him concerning every kind of medicine.
> — *Jubilees* 10:8–9, 12–13

## 4. The Book of Giants — *Dead Sea Scrolls, 3rd–2nd century BCE* → *the neighbours' heroes on the list*

Among the Dead Sea Scrolls are fragments of about **ten copies**, from four
caves (1Q23, 1Q24, 2Q26, 4Q203, 4Q206 2–3, 4Q530–533, 6Q8), of an Aramaic
**Book of Giants** — the story of Genesis 6 told from the giants' side. No
openly licensed English translation exists, so we describe it; the standard
editions are listed at the foot of the page.

- **The giants have names.** The two brothers **Ohyah** and **Hahyah**, sons
  of Shemiḥazah; a messenger giant called **Mahway**.
- **Two of the names come from Mesopotamia.** One giant is **Gilgamesh**
  (spelled *glgmys* in 4Q530, *glgmyš* in 4Q531) — the hero of the most
  famous epic of the ancient world. Another is **Ḥobabish** (4Q203 and
  4Q530) — **Humbaba**, the monster Gilgamesh killed. In one fragment Ohyah
  reports what Gilgamesh told him, and then Ḥobabish cries out, and judgment
  is pronounced against him (4Q530 2 ii line 2 — a damaged line, and the
  reading is tentative).
- **The giants dream of the flood.** Hahyah dreams of a great garden with
  gardeners watering it, and of **water and fire destroying it**, with three
  shoots growing from one root — read as Noah and his three sons. In another
  fragment, **waters rise over a tablet** and wash its writing away. Ohyah
  dreams of the Ruler of Heaven coming down: *"thrones were set up,"* the
  Great Holy One sits, and *"books were opened"* — the scene of Daniel 7:9–10.
- **They send to Enoch.** Mahway *"flew with his hands, like an eagle"* to ask
  Enoch, the scribe, what the dreams mean.

The book was later adopted by **Mani**, the third-century founder of
Manichaeism, and pieces of his version turned up in the early 1900s at
**Turfan** in Chinese Central Asia. Ḥobabish is there; Gilgamesh is not.

**Why it matters for this session.** Genesis 6:4 says the children of the
sons of God were *"the mighty heroes of old, the men of the name."* Two
centuries before Christ, Jewish scribes read that line and wrote **Gilgamesh**
into it. They heard the verse as the session suggests its first hearers did:
as a reference to **the neighbours' famous heroes**.

## 5. The Damascus Document — *c. 100 BCE* → *on the angels* · → *on the heart*

A rule-book of the Dead Sea community, known first from a medieval copy in
the Cairo Genizah and then from Qumran. R. H. Charles, 1913 (CD II 17–III 1):

> For many were led astray by them, / And mighty men of valour stumbled by
> them from of old [and until this day]. / **Because they walked in the
> stubbornness of their heart the watchers of heaven fell.** / By them were
> they caught because they kept not the commandment of God. / **And their
> children whose height was like the loftiness of the cedars / And whose
> bodies were like the mountains fell thereby.** / All flesh that was on dry
> land perished thereby, / And they were as though they had not been.

"Them" are *"the thoughts of an evil imagination and eyes of fornication"*
from the line before. Two things: the Watchers fell by **the stubbornness of
their heart** — the heart again, as in Genesis 6:5 — and their children were
*"like the loftiness of the cedars,"* borrowed from Amos 2:9, where it
describes the **Amorites** of Canaan. The writer has joined the giants before
the flood to the giants Israel met in the land.

## 6. The Apocrypha — *2nd–1st century BCE* → *toward the session's reading*

Five short passages from the books that Catholic and Orthodox Bibles include.
King James Version (1611) except where noted.

> **He was not pacified toward the old giants, who fell away in the strength of
> their foolishness.** — *Sirach* 16:7

Ben Sira wrote in Hebrew around 180 BCE, and his Hebrew survives for this
verse. It does not say "giants." It says ***nəsîkê qedem*** — **"the princes of
old"**: *"He forgave not the princes of old, / Who revolted in their might"*
(Box and Oesterley's translation of the Hebrew, 1913). His grandson, putting
it into Greek, made the princes "giants," following the Greek Genesis. So the
oldest Jewish comment we have on the Nephilim calls them **rulers** — which is
the grain of truth in the "kings" reading of the session's Part 2.

> For in the old time also, **when the proud giants perished, the hope of the
> world governed by thy hand escaped in a weak vessel**, and left to all ages
> a seed of generation. — *Wisdom of Solomon* 14:6

> There were the giants famous from the beginning, that were of so great
> stature, and so expert in war. **Those did not the Lord choose**, neither
> gave he the way of knowledge unto them: But they were destroyed, because they
> had no wisdom, and perished through their own foolishness. — *Baruch*
> 3:26–28

> Thou didst destroy those who aforetime did iniquity, **among whom were giants
> trusting in their strength and boldness, bringing upon them a boundless
> flood of water.** Thou didst burn up with fire and brimstone **the men of
> Sodom**, workers of arrogance… — *3 Maccabees* 2:4–5 (C. W. Emmet, 1913)

> For the mighty one did not fall by the young men, **neither did the sons of
> the Titans smite him, nor high giants set upon him**: but Judith the
> daughter of Merari weakened him with the beauty of her countenance.
> — *Judith* 16:7

*Wisdom* sets the "proud giants" beside **the ark** — *"the hope of the
world… in a weak vessel."* *Baruch* makes the giants the opposite of wisdom:
strength without knowledge of God. *3 Maccabees* runs **giants → flood →
Sodom**, the same order as 2 Peter 2:4–6. And *Judith*, reaching for words
for the most terrible warriors imaginable, puts **the sons of the Titans**
beside the giants — Greek myth and Genesis in one breath.

## 7. The Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs — *2nd century BCE – 2nd century CE* · ⇢ *and → by turns*

Farewell speeches put in the mouths of Jacob's twelve sons; the Jewish core
is old, but the text passed through Christian hands, and its date is
disputed. R. H. Charles (1917).

> 6. For thus they allured the Watchers who were before the flood; for as
> these continually beheld them, they lusted after them, and they conceived
> the act in their mind; **for they changed themselves into the shape of men**,
> and appeared to them when they were with their husbands. 7. And the women
> lusting in their minds after their forms, gave birth to giants…
> — *Testament of Reuben* 5:6–7

⇢ *Reuben* answers the question *how?* — the Watchers **took human shape** —
and then does something neither Genesis nor *1 Enoch* does: it **blames the
women**. It is worth noticing and worth refusing. In Genesis 6:2 the women
are acted upon; *they took… from any they chose*.

> 4. …that ye become not as **Sodom, which changed the order of nature**. 5.
> **In like manner the Watchers also changed the order of their nature, whom
> the Lord cursed at the flood**, on whose account He made the earth without
> inhabitant and fruitless. — *Testament of Naphtali* 3:4–5

→ This is the pairing **Jude** makes — Sodom and the angels, both leaving
their proper order, *"in a way similar to these angels"* (Jude 7).

## 8. Philo of Alexandria, *On the Giants* — *c. 20 BCE – 50 CE* ⇢ *a different direction*

Philo, a Jewish philosopher in Alexandria and an older contemporary of Paul,
wrote a whole treatise on Genesis 6:1–4. His Bible read *"angels of God."*
C. D. Yonge (1854):

> "And when the angels of God saw the daughters of men that they were
> beautiful, they took unto themselves wives of all of them whom they chose."
> **Those beings, whom other philosophers call demons, Moses usually calls
> angels; and they are souls hovering in the air. And let no one suppose, that
> what is here stated is a fable**… — §§6–7

> If, therefore, you consider that souls, and demons, and angels are things
> differing indeed in name, but one and identical in reality, you will then be
> able to discard that most heavy burden, superstition. — §16

> **Therefore he utters no fable whatever respecting the giants; but he wishes
> to set this fact before your eyes, that some men are born of the earth, and
> some are born of heaven, and some are born of God**: those are born of the
> earth, who are hunters after the pleasures of the body… — §60

Philo insists the passage is *not a fable* — and then reads it as an
allegory of the soul. His "giants" are people who live for the body. It is
the first of many attempts to keep the text and lose the embarrassment.

## 9. Josephus — *c. 93 CE* → *toward the session's reading*

Josephus retold Genesis for Romans, and he was blunt. William Whiston (1737):

> Now this posterity of Seth continued to esteem God as the Lord of the
> universe, and to have an entire regard to virtue, for seven generations: but
> in process of time they were perverted… **For many Angels of God accompanied
> with women, and begat sons that proved unjust, and despisers of all that was
> good; on account of the confidence they had in their own strength. For the
> tradition is, that these men did what resembled the acts of those whom the
> Grecians call Giants.** But Noah was very uneasy at what they did: and being
> displeased at their conduct, persuaded them to change their dispositions, and
> their actions for the better. But seeing they did not yield to him, but were
> slaves to their wicked pleasures, he was afraid they would kill him,
> together with his wife and children, and those they had married. So he
> departed out of that land. — *Antiquities* 1.3.1 (§§72–74)

Notice that Josephus has **Seth's line go bad** *and* **angels father the
giants** — the two readings that later split apart, still in one paragraph.
And he calls them what his Roman readers would recognise: **Giants**, like the
Greeks'. Whiston's footnote (1737): *"This notion, that the fallen Angels
were, in some sense, the fathers of the old Giants, was the constant opinion
of antiquity."*

Later, at Hebron, the last of them:

> There were till then left the race of giants; who had bodies so large, and
> countenances so intirely different from other men, that they were surprising
> to the sight, and terrible to the hearing. **The bones of these men are still
> shewn to this very day**; unlike to any credible relations of other men.
> — *Antiquities* 5.2.3 (§125)

What those bones were, the [neighbours sheet](the-neighbors.html) suggests.

## 10. The New Testament

The letters of Jude and 2 Peter were written in this world, to readers who
knew these books. The verses are printed in full on the [Scripture
sheet](elsewhere-in-scripture.html); here is how they line up.

| | *1 Enoch* / *Jubilees* | The New Testament |
|---|---|---|
| **Left their place** | "ye have left the high, holy, and eternal heaven" (*1 En.* 15:3) | "abandoned their own place of residence" (Jude 6) |
| **Bound in darkness** | "cast him into the darkness… let him abide there" (*1 En.* 10:4–5) | "in eternal chains in utter darkness" (Jude 6); "in chains in utter darkness" (2 Pet 2:4) |
| **Until the judgment** | "till the day of their judgement" (*1 En.* 10:12) | "locked up for the judgment of the great Day" (Jude 6); "kept until the judgment" (2 Pet 2:4) |
| **In the days of Noah** | the flood as God's answer (*1 En.* 10:2) | "disobedient long ago… in the days of Noah" (1 Pet 3:20); angels, then the flood (2 Pet 2:4–5) |
| **Beside Sodom** | "Sodom… In like manner the Watchers" (*T. Naph.* 3:4–5); giants, flood, Sodom (*3 Macc.* 2:4–5) | "Sodom and Gomorrah… in a way similar to these angels" (Jude 7); angels, flood, Sodom (2 Pet 2:4–6) |
| **Enoch quoted** | "He cometh with ten thousands of His holy ones" (*1 En.* 1:9) | "The Lord is coming with thousands and thousands of his holy ones" (Jude 14) |
{: .ff-wrap}

One word more. Where the NET says God *"threw them into hell"* (2 Peter 2:4),
the Greek is a single verb, ***tartarōsas*** — "**cast them into
Tartarus**." In Greek myth, Tartarus was the pit where the **Titans**, who
fought the gods of heaven, were chained. Peter chose the word his readers
would know for the prison of heaven's rebels.

## Provenance and licensing

- **The Greek Bible (Septuagint)** — Greek, 3rd–2nd century BCE, **public
  domain**. English: **Sir Lancelot C. L. Brenton**, *The Septuagint Version
  of the Old Testament* (London, 1851) — **public domain** — via
  [eBible.org](https://ebible.org/eng-Brenton/GEN06.htm); the "angels of God"
  reading of Codex Alexandrinus is from Brenton's own note and H. B. Swete,
  *The Old Testament in Greek* (Cambridge, 1887), both public domain; the
  readings of Aquila, Symmachus and Theodotion, and the quotation from Cyril
  of Alexandria, from Frederick Field, *Origenis Hexaplorum quae supersunt*
  (Oxford, 1875), **public domain**, English glosses ours. The Göttingen
  critical texts of Genesis (J. W. Wevers, 1974) and Deuteronomy (1977) are
  cited for the majority readings, not quoted. 4QDeut<sup>j</sup> (4Q37),
  *DJD* XIV (1995), cited.
- ***1 Enoch*** — Aramaic original, 3rd century BCE. English: **R. H.
  Charles**, *The Book of Enoch* (London: SPCK, 1917), **public domain**, via
  [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Enoch_(Charles)).
  Quoted verbatim except that we have **removed Charles's text-critical
  brackets** (the marks showing which words are only in the Greek or only in
  the Ethiopic); bold emphasis is ours, and ellipses mark omissions. The Greek
  "three kinds" of giants at 7:2 is quoted from Charles's note in *The Book of
  Enoch or 1 Enoch* (Oxford, 1912), public domain.
- ***Jubilees*** — Hebrew original, c. 160–150 BCE. English: **R. H.
  Charles**, *The Book of Jubilees* (London: SPCK, 1917), **public domain**,
  via [sacred-texts.com](https://www.sacred-texts.com/bib/jub/) (archived
  copies) and [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Book_of_Jubilees.5), with
  Charles's verse numbers (Sefaria numbers the verses differently). The "[My]"
  in 5:8 corrects a misprint, following Charles's 1913 text. Bold emphasis is
  ours.
- **The Book of Giants** — Aramaic, 3rd–2nd century BCE. **Described, not
  quoted.** Standard editions: J. T. Milik, *The Books of Enoch: Aramaic
  Fragments of Qumrân Cave 4* (Oxford, 1976); L. T. Stuckenbruck, *The Book of
  Giants from Qumran* (Tübingen, 1997); É. Puech, *DJD* XXXI (2001); I. Marro
  Sánchez, *The Book of Giants: A Critical Edition of the Aramaic Fragments*
  (dissertation, LMU Munich, 2026), from which the fragment references and the
  reading of 4Q530 2 ii 2 are taken. The Manichaean fragments: W. B. Henning,
  *Bulletin of the School of Oriental and African Studies* 11 (1943): 52–74.
- **The Damascus Document** — Hebrew, c. 100 BCE. English: **R. H. Charles**,
  "Fragments of a Zadokite Work," in *The Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the
  Old Testament*, vol. 2 (Oxford, 1913), 3:3–4:1 = CD II 17–III 1 — **public
  domain** — via [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/apocryphapseudep02charuoft).
  Slashes mark Charles's line breaks.
- **Sirach, Wisdom, Baruch, Judith** — King James Version with Apocrypha
  (1611), **public domain**, via [eBible.org](https://ebible.org/eng-kjv/).
  The Hebrew of Sirach 16:7 is from the Cairo Genizah manuscript as edited by
  S. Schechter and C. Taylor (Cambridge, 1899), public domain, via
  [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Ben_Sira.16.7); the English of the Hebrew
  is **G. H. Box and W. O. E. Oesterley** in *The Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha
  of the Old Testament*, vol. 1 (1913), public domain. **3 Maccabees**:
  **C. W. Emmet**, in the same volume (1913), public domain.
- **Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs** — English: **R. H. Charles**, *The
  Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs* (London: SPCK, 1917), **public
  domain**, via [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/thetestamentsoft00unknuoft).
- **Philo, *On the Giants*** — Greek, early 1st century CE. English: **C. D.
  Yonge**, *The Works of Philo Judaeus*, vol. 1 (London: Bohn, 1854) —
  **public domain** — via [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/worksphilojudaeu01philuoft),
  with obvious scanning errors corrected; section numbers are the standard
  ones. (A widely copied online "updated" Yonge misprints §16 as "not
  identical"; the 1854 text reads "one and identical.")
- **Josephus, *Antiquities of the Jews*** — Greek, c. 93 CE. English:
  **William Whiston**, 1737 — **public domain** — via the [University of
  Chicago's Penelope archive](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/josephus/ant-1.html).
  Quoted verbatim, retaining Whiston's spelling; bold emphasis is ours.
