## The shape of the session

Five parts and a close, the same rhythm as Session 7:

1. **Read it slowly** — *saw → good → took* is Genesis 3:6 exactly, and then
   the rulers' grammar (12:15; 34:2; 2 Sam 11:2–4); *ʿōlām* in 6:3 = 3:22;
   *ʾanšê haššēm*, "men of the name," as an honour roll; no one is called tall.
2. **Who are the sons of God?** — the "son of X = class X" idiom; the eight
   verses (all heavenly outside Genesis); the divine council (1 Kgs 22, Isa 6,
   Dan 7, Ps 82, Ps 148:5, Neh 9:6; Ugarit's "sons of El" and seventy sons of
   Athirat; Deut 32:8 in 4QDeut<sup>j</sup>, which the NET follows); the three
   readings in a table; lands **with Jude / 2 Peter** (per the standing
   decision that NT readings are authoritative for the class — see memory
   "Hebrews authoritative; source question both sides"), giving the Seth and
   king readings their strongest case first.
3. **The Nephilim** — *gigantes* was a Greek word for earth-born rebels
   against heaven, not "tall people"; size enters via the spies' *dibbâ*
   (evil report), which Rahab later shows was wrong; the thread Nephilim →
   Anakim → Rephaim → Og → Gath → Goliath ends with David; Goliath's height
   (MT vs 4QSam<sup>a</sup>/LXX — the NET says "close to seven feet");
   **Rephaim = the royal dead** (Ugarit KTU 1.108: Rapiʾu at Ashtaroth and
   Edrei = Og's cities; Phoenician coffin curses); ancient "evidence"
   (Anastasi I, Gath's walls, fossil bones); how Og survived the flood.
4. **How the story grew** — Gilgamesh (two-thirds god, "make a name"),
   Hesiod's demigods and Zeus's plan to end them; *1 Enoch*'s Watchers (the
   *apkallu* curriculum as stolen goods; Hermon = Og's mountain); the Book of
   Giants names **Gilgamesh and Ḥobabish**; what Genesis leaves out; *1 Enoch*
   blames the angels ("to him ascribe all sin"), Genesis blames the heart.
5. **Two hearts** (6:5–8) — three "saws"; *yēṣer* from *yāṣar*; *his heart*
   ends both 6:5 and 6:6; **Lamech's two words (5:29, *n-ḥ-m* and *ʿ-ṣ-b*)
   return as God's grief**; Isa 63:10 / Eph 4:30; 1 Sam 15's both-hands
   answer; *Genesis Rabbah* 27:4 (the son's birth; seven days of mourning;
   David over Absalom); *Noah* / *ḥēn* anagram, first "grace" in the Bible,
   before "righteous" (and the rabbis: "not that he was worthy").

**So what?** — Genesis won't let the monsters take the blame; the Son of God
crosses the line the other way (Gal 4:4, John 1:12, Phil 2:6–9 — flagged in
the text as our line of thought); God's first feeling is pain; grace before
righteousness.

## Verification notes worth keeping

Research was done 2026-09-25 by four parallel agents, each quoting only from
pages actually fetched, then script-checked against the saved source. Things
that surprised us or corrected the brief:

- **Hebrew counts** (checked against the whole MT from Sefaria): *bənê
  hāʾĕlōhîm / ʾēlîm / ʿelyôn* = exactly Gen 6:2, 4; Job 1:6; 2:1; 38:7; Ps 29:1;
  82:6; 89:7 (Heb). *Nəpilîm* as a noun = Gen 6:4 and Num 13:33 only. *ʾanšê
  haššēm* = Gen 6:4 and Num 16:2 only. The **hithpael of *ʿ-ṣ-b*** = Gen 6:6 and
  34:7 only (45:5 and Neh 8:10–11 are niphal). *Ḥēn* first occurs at Gen 6:8.
- **NET smooths the key words**: *ṭōbōt* → "beautiful"; *bāśār* → "mortal";
  *ʾanšê haššēm* → "famous men"; *wayyitʿaṣṣēb* → "he was highly offended"
  (hides the pain root; so does Isa 63:10 "offended"); Dan 3:25 *bar ʾĕlāhîn* →
  "like that of a god." The handout and passage sheet give the literal Hebrew.
- **Genesis Rabbah 26:5**: R. Shimon bar Yoḥai says "sons of the judges" and
  curses anyone who calls them "sons of God" — the text never says "angels."
  The bride/nobleman practice is **R. Yudan's**, not his.
- **Genesis Rabbah 27:4**: the heretic's objection is foreknowledge vs "he was
  saddened in His heart"; the "time of rejoicing / time of mourning" line is
  the heretic's own; R. Yehoshua ben Levi: God mourned seven days, proved from
  2 Sam 19:3 Heb (= 19:2 Eng), David over Absalom.
- **Targum Pseudo-Jonathan 6:4** names "Shamḥazai and **Uzziel**," not Azael.
  *Yoma* 67b says only "Uzza and Azael"; the Davidson English's link to Gen 6
  is Steinsaltz's gloss. *Niddah* 61a's Shamḥazai genealogy is anonymous.
- **Ramban on 6:4** concedes the fallen-angel reading of PRE 22 "fits into the
  language of the verse more than all other interpretations." **Rashi on 6:2**
  keeps angels as his second explanation. **Ibn Ezra** is the only Jewish text
  found that mentions the Seth/Cain reading ("others say").
- **Sirach 16:7** in Hebrew says *nəsîkê qedem*, "princes of old," not giants
  (Schechter–Taylor 1899; Box–Oesterley 1913).
- **1 Enoch 7:2**: "three thousand ells" is in both Greek and Ethiopic; the
  variant is Syncellus's three kinds (giants → Naphilim → Eliud) = Jub 7:22.
  Quote Charles 1917 from **Wikisource**; the CCEL copy mangles the names.
- **Jubilees**: Sefaria's verse numbers are not Charles's (and Sefaria prints
  "Nâphîlîm" at 7:22 where Charles has "Nâphîdîm"). The Jared/Watchers verse is
  **4:15** in Charles — note that **Session 7's earliest-readings sheet numbers
  it 4:17–18** (it followed Sefaria). Worth a quiet fix there.
- **Philo**: the online "updated Yonge" misprints §16 as "not identical"; the
  1854 print reads "one and identical."
- **Book of Giants**: no openly licensed English exists — described only.
  Gilgamesh = *glgmys* (4Q530) / *glgmyš* (4Q531 22 12); Ḥobabish = 4Q203 3 3,
  4Q530 2 ii 2. Gilgamesh is **not** in the Turfan Manichaean fragments;
  Ḥobabish is. Best current edition: Marro Sánchez, LMU Munich diss. (2026).
- **Julius Africanus** (c. 221) is the first Christian Sethite — and his own
  Bible read "angels of heaven." The Seth view spread through the Syriac church
  in the 4th c. before Augustine. **Keil** (Sethite) concedes the angel reading
  is "most natural" apart from context; **Delitzsch** held the angel view.
  **Luther** is PD (Lenker 1910, Gutenberg #27978). Calvin dismisses both the
  angels ("figment") and the Targum's nobles ("frigid").
- Could not verify: Chrysostom calling the angel view "absurd" (say
  "rejects"); the "holy mountain" motif in Ephrem himself (it is *Cave of
  Treasures*); Kidner's and Hamilton's positions (dropped).

## Threads handed forward to Sessions 9 and 10 (the doublets, then the Flood)

- **6:9–13 tells the reason for the flood a second time** — *God* not *the
  LORD*, *violence/corruption* not *the heart*, and 6:12 inverts 1:31 (*God
  saw… and behold it was ruined*). The handout ends Part 5 pointing here. It is
  the natural place for the flood-doublets source question (both sides, per
  the standing decision).
- **The LORD's heart**: grieved at 6:6, speaks *"to his heart"* at 8:21 — and
  what he says is mercy, citing the same *yēṣer* diagnosis as 6:5.
- **The list of creatures** announced in 6:7 is executed word for word at 7:23.
- **Og on the ark** (PsJ Gen 14:13; *Niddah* 61a; PRE 23:8) and the boiling
  flood waters (*Zevachim* 113b; *Sanhedrin* 108a) are on the rabbinic sheet.
- **1 Peter 3:19–20** ("spirits in prison… in the days of Noah") and Grudem's
  "Christ preaching through Noah" reading belong to Noah, not only to 6:1–4.
