# Genesis 12:1–9 in Hebrew

A source sheet for **[Session 12](../)**. It gives the passage, with the two
verses before it and the one after, and fourteen notes on what the Hebrew does
that an English translation cannot easily show. All counts are from the Open
Scriptures Hebrew Bible (the Westminster Leningrad Codex, tagged); lexicon
glosses are from Brown–Driver–Briggs (1906).

---

## Haran: Genesis 11:31–32

> **31** Terah took his son Abram, his grandson Lot (the son of Haran), and his
> daughter-in-law Sarai, his son Abram's wife, and with them he set out from Ur
> of the Chaldeans to go to Canaan. When they came to Haran, they settled
> there. **32** The lifetime of Terah was 205 years, and he died in Haran.

## The call and the road: Genesis 12:1–9

> **1** Now the LORD said to Abram, "Go out from your country, your relatives,
> and your father's household to the land that I will show you. **2** Then I
> will make you into a great nation, and I will bless you, and I will make your
> name great, so that you will exemplify divine blessing. **3** I will bless
> those who bless you, but the one who treats you lightly I must curse, so that
> all the families of the earth may receive blessing through you."
>
> **4** So Abram left, just as the LORD had told him to do, and Lot went with
> him. (Now Abram was 75 years old when he departed from Haran.) **5** And
> Abram took his wife Sarai, his nephew Lot, and all the possessions they had
> accumulated and the people they had acquired in Haran, and they left for the
> land of Canaan. They entered the land of Canaan.
>
> **6** Abram traveled through the land as far as the oak tree of Moreh at
> Shechem. (At that time the Canaanites were in the land.) **7** The LORD
> appeared to Abram and said, "To your descendants I will give this land." So
> Abram built an altar there to the LORD, who had appeared to him. **8** Then
> he moved from there to the hill country east of Bethel and pitched his tent,
> with Bethel on the west and Ai on the east. There he built an altar to the
> LORD and worshiped the LORD. **9** Abram continually journeyed by stages down
> to the Negev.

## Next: Genesis 12:10

> **10** There was a famine in the land, so Abram went down to Egypt to stay
> for a while because the famine was severe.

---

## Fourteen notes

### 1. *Lek-ləḵā*: "Go, yourself"

*"Go out"* (12:1) is ***lek-ləḵā***, literally "go, for yourself" or "go,
yourself" — the verb *go* followed by *to you*. The two words are written with
the same four letters, לך לך. The masculine form occurs **twice in the whole
Bible**: here, and when God says to Abraham *"Take your son… and go
(**wə-lek-ləḵā**) to the land of Moriah… on one of the mountains which I will
tell you"* (22:2; the NET has *"indicate to you"*). Both times the place is not
named until he gets there:

| **12:1** | **22:2** |
|---|---|
| *lek-ləḵā* — "go, yourself" | *wə-lek-ləḵā* — "and go, yourself" |
| *"to the land **that I will show you**"* (*ʾăšer ʾarʾekkā*) | *"on one of the mountains **that I will tell you**"* (*ʾăšer ʾōmar ʾēleyḵā*) |
| leave your father | give up your son |
{: .ff-wrap}

The feminine form, *lᵊḵî-lāḵ*, is the Song of Songs' *"come away"*: *"Arise, my
darling; my beautiful one, come away with me!"* (Song 2:10, 13). Rashi reads the
*to you* as *for you*: *"for your own benefit and for your own good"* (on the
**[readers' sheet](early-readers.html)**).

The three things Abram leaves — *"your country, your relatives, and your
father's household"* — narrow as they go: the land, the clan, the house.

### 2. "I will show you" … "the LORD appeared"

Three words, one root, ***rāʾâ***, "see":

| | **Hebrew** | **Form** | **Literally** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12:1 | *ʾarʾekkā* | causative (hiphil), future | "I will make you see" |
| 12:7 | *wayyērāʾ* | reflexive (niphal), past | "and [the LORD] let himself be seen" |
| 12:7 | *hannirʾeh* | reflexive participle | "[the LORD] who had let himself be seen" |
{: .ff-wrap}

- **This is the first time God "appears" in the Bible.** Genesis 1–11 uses the
  niphal of *rāʾâ* three times — the dry land (1:9), the mountain tops (8:5),
  the rainbow (9:14) — and never of God. From 12:7 on, God *appears* nine
  times in Genesis: 12:7 (twice), 17:1, 18:1, 26:2, 26:24, 35:1, 35:9, 48:3.
  (35:7 is a different verb, *niḡlû*, "revealed himself.")
- ***"Who had appeared"*** comes only twice in Genesis, and both times it names
  the God an altar is for: Abram's altar *"to the LORD, who had appeared to
  him"* (12:7), and God's command to Jacob, *"Make an altar there to God, who
  appeared to you"* (35:1).
- **God says "I show you" only twice in the Torah,** first and last, and both
  times about this land: *"to the land that I will show you"* (Genesis 12:1),
  and to Moses on Mount Nebo, *"This is the land I promised to Abraham, Isaac,
  and Jacob when I said, 'I will give it to your descendants.' I have let you
  see it (**herʾîṯîḵā**), but you will not cross over there"* (Deuteronomy
  34:4) — quoting God's words at Shechem, *"to your descendants I will give
  it."*
- The root keeps going in the next scene, but people do the seeing: Sarai is
  *"beautiful in appearance"* (*mar'eh*, 12:11), *"when the Egyptians see
  you"* (12:12), *"the Egyptians saw"* (12:14), *"Pharaoh's officials saw
  her"* (12:15). Seven times in the chapter.

### 3. The same sentence, two endings

Terah's journey (11:31) and Abram's (12:5) are told in the same words until the
last three:

| **11:31** | | **12:5** |
|---|---|---|
| *wayyēṣʾû* — and they went out | = | *wayyēṣʾû* — and they went out |
| *ʾittām mēʾûr kaśdîm* — with them from Ur of the Chaldeans | | — |
| *lāleḵeṯ ʾarṣâ kənaʿan* — to go to the land of Canaan | = | *lāleḵeṯ ʾarṣâ kənaʿan* — to go to the land of Canaan |
| *wayyāḇōʾû* — and they came | = | *wayyāḇōʾû* — and they came |
| ***ʿaḏ-ḥārān wayyēšəḇû šām*** — as far as Haran, and they settled there | ≠ | ***ʾarṣâ kənāʿan*** — to the land of Canaan |
{: .ff-wrap}

### 4. "They settled there" — and never again

***Wayyēšəḇû šām***, "and they settled there" (11:31), is word for word what
the builders did in Shinar (11:2). In 11:31–12:9 the verbs of motion pile up —
*go* (*hālaḵ*) six times (11:31; 12:1; 12:4 twice; 12:5; 12:9), *go out* three
times, *come* twice, *pull up stakes* twice, *pass through* once, *move on*
once — and *settle* (*yāšaḇ*) only once, of Terah's party. Abram's party never
settles.

### 5. Abram says nothing

*"So Abram left, just as the LORD had told him to do"* (12:4) is ***wayyēleḵ ʾaḇrām
kaʾăšer dibber ʾēlāyw YHWH*** — and the verse goes on *wayyēleḵ ʾittô lôṭ*, "and
Lot went with him": two *went*s answering God's *go*. Every verb of speaking
in 11:26–12:10 has the LORD as its subject (12:1, 4, 7). Abram's first recorded
words come at 12:11–13, on the way into Egypt: *"Look, I know that you are a
beautiful woman…"*

### 6. "The souls they had made"

*"The people they had acquired in Haran"* is ***wəʾeṯ-hannep̄eš ʾăšer-ʿāśû
ḇəḥārān***, literally "the soul that they had made in Haran" — *nep̄eš*
singular, standing for a group, and the ordinary verb *make*. Just before it is
a matching pair: ***rəḵûšām ʾăšer rāḵāšû***, "their gains that they had
gained." Rashi gives the midrash first — *"The souls which he had brought
beneath the sheltering wings of the Shechinah. Abraham converted the men and
Sarah converted the women and Scripture accounts it unto them as if they had
made them"* (from *Genesis Rabbah* 39:14) — and then *"the real sense of the
text"*: servants they had acquired, as *made* means *acquired* in 31:1. The
Aramaic Targum Onkelos had already translated the verse the midrash's way:
*"the souls whom they had made subject to the law in Charan."*

### 7. The oak of Moreh

*"The oak tree of Moreh"* is ***ʾēlôn môreh***. *Môreh* is the participle of
*yārâ* in its causative sense, "point out, show, direct, teach" — "teacher."
Brown–Driver–Briggs glosses the phrase *"the teacher's terebinth near
Shechem… the terebinth being a holy tree from which divine teaching was
given."* The name occurs three times: here; *"the oaks of Moreh"* beside Mount
Gerizim and Mount Ebal (Deuteronomy 11:30); and *"the hill of Moreh"* in the
Jezreel valley (Judges 7:1). Near Shechem stood another named tree, *ʾēlôn
məʿônənîm*, *"the Oak Tree of the Diviners"* (Judges 9:37), and Joshua set up
his covenant stone *"under the oak tree near the LORD's sanctuary"* at Shechem
(Joshua 24:26), where Jacob had buried his household's idols *"under the oak
near Shechem"* (35:4).

***Tôrâ***, *Torah*, comes from the same root — *instruction*, the thing a
*môreh* gives. Rashi: at the oak of Moreh God *"showed him Mount Gerizim and
Mount Ebal where Israel took upon themselves the oath to observe the
Torah."*

### 8. "At that time the Canaanites were in the land"

***Wəhakkənaʿănî ʾāz bāʾāreṣ*** — "and the Canaanite [was] then in the land,"
with no verb. Two old readings:

- **Rashi:** the Canaanites *"were gradually conquering the land of Israel
  from the descendants of Shem"* — so the promise in 12:7 is a restoration.
- **Abraham ibn Ezra** (12th century), in Hebrew: *"It is possible that Canaan
  seized the land of Canaan from another. If not, there is a secret here, and
  the one who understands will keep silent"* (our translation). His
  translators explain the hint: *then* sounds like a narrator writing after the
  Canaanites were gone. Readers who hear two strands in Genesis (Session 9)
  take this as one of the narrator's later asides; readers who hold to one
  author take *then* the way Rashi does — the Canaanites were *already* there,
  so the land Abram is promised is not empty.

### 9. The altars

***Mizbēaḥ***, "altar," appears thirteen times in Genesis. The first is Noah's
(8:20). Abram builds four: at Shechem (12:7), between Bethel and Ai (12:8; he
comes back to it in 13:4), at Mamre by Hebron (13:18), and on Moriah (22:9).
Jacob builds his two at the same places as Abram's first two: Shechem (33:20)
and Bethel (35:7).

### 10. "He called on the name of the LORD"

The NET's *"worshiped the LORD"* (12:8) is ***wayyiqrāʾ bəšēm YHWH***, "and he
called on (or proclaimed) the name of the LORD" — the words of 4:26, *"then
people began to call on the name of the LORD."* Genesis has the phrase five
times: 4:26; 12:8; 13:4; 21:33; 26:25 (the NET renders all five *worship*). The
same words, with God as the speaker, are his own self-proclamation at Sinai:
*"he proclaimed the LORD by name"* (Exodus 34:5). The builders of 11:4 wanted to
make a name for themselves; Abram, at his second altar, calls on God's.

### 11. "His tent" — or "her tent"?

*"And pitched his tent"* (12:8) writes *his tent* as ***ʾohŏlōh***, with a
final *h* — an old spelling of *his*, but the same letters as *ʾohŏlāh*, "her
tent." The midrash reads it both ways at once: *"It teaches that he pitched
Sarah's tent first, and then pitched his tent"* (*Genesis Rabbah* 39:15); Rashi
repeats it. The same spelling comes at 9:21, 13:3 and 35:21. (Some printed
Bibles mark a written-and-read variant here; the Leningrad Codex does so only
at 13:3. The midrash rests on the spelling, not on a variant.)

### 12. "Going and pulling up stakes"

*"Abram continually journeyed by stages down to the Negev"* (12:9) is
***wayyissaʿ ʾaḇrām hālôḵ wənāsôaʿ hannegbâ***: "and Abram pulled up, going and
pulling up, toward the Negev." The verb *nāsaʿ* first means "pull out or up" —
tent pegs (Isaiah 33:20) — and so "set out, journey by stages." The doubled
infinitives (*hālôḵ wə-…*, "going and…") are the Hebrew way of saying *on and
on*. Genesis uses the idiom four times: the flood waters *"kept receding"*
(8:3) and *"kept on receding"* (8:5), Abram here, and Isaac whose *"influence
continued to grow"* (26:13). And *Negev*, "the dry land," is also the ordinary
word for *south*.

### 13. "Land," seven times

***ʾEreṣ***, "land," comes seven times in 12:1–9: twice in verse 1 (*"from
your land… to the land"*), twice in verse 5, twice in verse 6, once in verse 7
(*"this land"*). It is the word the passage is about. *Bless* comes five times
in verses 2–3 (Session 11).

### 14. Terah's years

Terah was 70 when his sons were born (11:26); Abram was 75 when he left Haran
(12:4); Terah died at 205 (11:32). If Abram was born when Terah was 70, Terah
was 145 when Abram left, and lived sixty more years in Haran. Rashi does the
same sum, and says Terah's death is told *before* Abram's call so that people
would not say *"Abram did not show a son's respect to his father"* (on the
**[readers' sheet](early-readers.html)**). Stephen says Abram moved on *"after
his father died"* (Acts 7:4). Two ways readers have reconciled these: Abram,
named first in 11:26 as the most important son, may have been born later —
when Terah was 130; and the Samaritan Pentateuch gives Terah 145 years, not
205, so that Abram leaves the year his father dies.

And one word in 11:30: *"she had no children"* is ***ʾên lāh wālāḏ***, "there
was not to her a child." *Wālāḏ* occurs only here in the standard text. The
section of seventy nations ends on a couple with none.
