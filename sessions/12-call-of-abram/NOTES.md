## The thread

*"To the land that I will show you"* (12:1, ***ʾarʾekkā***, hiphil of ראה) →
*"The LORD appeared"* (12:7, ***wayyērāʾ***, niphal of ראה): the promise of
seeing, and the seeing, are the same verb, with 400+ miles of road between
them (Haran → Shechem). The map carries the same thread: a star at Haran, a
star at Shechem, and the gap labelled between them.

Shape: (1) *Settled halfway* — 11:31, the family already on the road to
Canaan and stopped in Haran (*wayyēšəḇû šām* = Babel 11:2); Stephen's earlier
call in Mesopotamia (Acts 7:2–4; Gen 15:7) — so a call repeated to a man who
had stalled; Jubilees 12 (Abram's prayer in Haran; Terah wants to see the land
first). (2) *"The land that I will show you"* — Rashi/GR 39:9 (hidden to make
it dear and to reward every step), R. Huna (suspense then revelation: 22:2,
Jonah 3:2), Philo on the future tense, Hebrews 11:8; the 11:31 / 12:5 parallel.
(3) *"The LORD appeared"* — Moreh "teacher/one who points" (oracle tree?), the
Canaanites in the land, *wayyērāʾ* as the first theophany "appearance" in the
Bible; what is shown is a promise, not a map (Acts 7:5). (4) *An altar, a
tent, and the road* — altars vs Babel's tower; *hālôḵ wənāsôaʿ*; Ramban's
*maʿaseh avot siman la-banim*, Rashi's Abram praying ahead at Shechem and Ai;
Isaiah 51:2 to the exiles, and those who stayed (Josephus). Then *Next week*
(12:10: no word from God, no altar, Egypt — the guard-rail on the
application), *So what?*, a closing discussion box, and the map page.

**The conservative-reading contrast** chosen: the "lightning bolt" picture of
instant obedience. Genesis 11:31 and Acts 7:2–4 together tell of a man who
heard God, set out, and stopped halfway with his family; God called again. Not
raised (judged off-thread or divisive): the Christian-Zionist reading of 12:3;
"Canaan" as heaven in hymnody (Hebrews 11:13–16 reads it that way, and the
class takes Hebrews as authoritative).

**Where the rabbis go** (David asked): mostly *with* the application — the
land hidden "to make it more endearing to him, and to give him a reward for
each and every stride" (R. Yoḥanan, GR 39:9), and the **Zohar** (I:77b–78a)
says outright that the call came only after the family had set out "to go to
the land of Canaan," i.e. after Abram made the first move. Their own emphases,
worth knowing: (a) **the not-knowing keeps your eyes on God** — Rav Huna, GR
39:9: God "withholds… and keeps the eyes of the righteous directed toward Him"
(in the handout); (b) **the showing took away his fear** — Ramban on 12:6
(in the handout); (c) **the seeker came first** — GR 39:1's traveller "passing
from place to place" who sees the lit/burning palace, and GR 30:10's friend
holding a light for the king from a window in Mesopotamia; *Jubilees* 12 has
Abram praying in Haran for "the right path"; (d) **it was a trial, and the
hardest kind** — Avot 5:3; PRE 26 counts it third ("Migration is harder for
man than for any other creature"), *Midrash HaGadol* first and hardest; the
first and last trials both begin *lek-ləḵā* to an unnamed place (12:1; 22:2);
(e) ***lek-ləḵā* = "go for yourself"** — Rashi: for your own good (here no
children; there a nation); the Hasidic "go to yourself" (Zohar, *Mei
HaShiloach*); and *Sefat Emet*: the call "go" goes out to everyone always —
Abraham was the one listening. Philo noticed the future tense ("which I *will*
show") — on the sheet, cut from the handout for space.

**Hebrew facts (OSHB, all by code):** 12:7 *wayyērāʾ* is the first niphal of
ראה with God as subject (Gen 1–11 has only 1:9, 8:5, 9:14); God *appears* 9×
in Genesis; 35:7 is *niḡlû*, not ראה. Hiphil 1cs of ראה with God speaking: in
the Torah only Gen 12:1 and Deut 34:4 (which quotes 12:7). *Lek-ləḵā* (masc.)
only 12:1 and 22:2. 11:31 and 12:5 identical through *wayyāḇōʾû*. Abram
silent through 12:10; first words 12:11–13. הלך ×6 in 11:31–12:9; *yāšaḇ* ×1
(Terah's party). Altars: 13 tokens in Genesis; Abram builds 4 (12:7, 12:8,
13:18, 22:9). *ʾohŏlōh* 12:8: Leningrad has **no** ketiv/qere (only 13:3 does);
BHS marks one — the "her tent" midrash rests on the spelling. *ʾereṣ* ×7 in
12:1–9. *Wālāḏ* (11:30) only here in L.

**Licensing decisions.** Ramban: the Chavel translation is **CC BY** on Sefaria
(version "…Translated and annotated by Charles B. Chavel. New York, Shilo Pub.

from CCEL (NPNF 1887), not New Advent (modernised, claimed). Josephus from
penelope.uchicago.edu (Gutenberg has OCR slips here). Sefaria's Genesis Rabbah
39:9 English has two typos, corrected on the sheet.

**A correction worth remembering:** the popular "reward for every step" line is
Rabbi Yoḥanan in *Genesis Rabbah* 39:9 (*kol pəsîʿâ ûp̄əsîʿâ*). Rashi's version
(on 12:1) rewards *"each and every command"* (*dibbur*) — don't attribute the
"step" to Rashi.

**Geography.** Coordinates from Pleiades (CC BY). Ur→Haran ≈ 1,000 km / 620 mi
along the usual Euphrates waypoints (the map says "about 600"); Haran→Shechem
685–725 km / 425–450 mi depending on the route (map: "about 450"; handout:
"more than four hundred"). The Haran–Shechem leg is drawn dashed because
Genesis names no stops. Not verified: the three atlases (Aharoni & Avi-Yonah,
Rasmussen, Beitzel) — the sheet does not claim to follow any of them;
Westermann and Sarna on Moreh were seen only through a secondary quotation, so
they are described, not quoted; Wenham on Moreh was not found. Laban = "moon"
has no scholarly proponent and is left out.

## Panel, round 1 (2026-10-28)

Five independent seats. **Five of five** flagged "Settled halfway… God said it
again" as over-harmonised, from five directions: Stephen never says God spoke
twice, and the KJV/NIV "had said" readers would feel caricatured (evangelical);
11:31 and 12:5 are both P, which has no call — the "stop then the word" exists
only across the seam (modern Jewish); Genesis has Terah lead, Ramban says Abram
followed (mainline); Acts 7:4 vs the 60 years left unsaid (skeptic); the
tradition reads Abram's Haran as faithful waiting, "not yet permitted," GR
39:8 (rabbi). **Four** caught the Zohar contradicting Stephen on who moved
first, and "Often the first step is how we get the map" as self-help.

Changes made: §1 retitled *A stop in Haran*; Terah leads; Haran = "road"
(Akk. *ḫarrānu*); Stephen's earlier word, the KJV/NIV "had said", and Acts 7:4
named in one parenthesis; "some stops are faithful waiting… some are love" with
GR 39:7's *"I do not exempt anyone else"* ("staying beside someone who needs you
is not Haran"); the Zohar paragraph cut from the handout (kept on the sheet,
with the Stephen disagreement noted); Hebrews 11:8 given in order (call,
obedience, not knowing); "for us the word is seldom a voice — more often
something Scripture already makes plain"; Rashi's plain sense of "souls"
(servants) beside the midrash's converts, and "most of them walked this road
because someone else heard the word"; Sarai named; the Canaanites "already
someone's home" with GR 39:15; "first time *Genesis* says God appeared";
"from the same root as *Torah*"; "nearly word for word" with *Terah took /
Abram took*; Rashi at Shechem quoted whole (praying for the children's
failures); "the rabbis' saying, which Ramban made a rule"; Josephus on the
Babylon stayers cut from the handout (rabbi: it makes Babylonian Jewry into
Terahs) — the exile point is now the modern Jewish seat's: "Ur of the
Chaldeans" was Babylon to the exiles; *Next week* now "Running on fear, with a
scheme" (Ramban's charge is "he should have trusted," not "no word"); So what?
#2 adds Psalm 119:105 ("a lamp, not a floodlight"), #4 adds 12:3 as the test.
Sheets: Hebrew note 2 (God speaks of showing *the land* twice; Exod 25:9;
Deut 34:4 echoes Exod 33:1, Gen 24:7 repeats 12:7), note 3 (Terah took / Abram
took; a two-strands paragraph), note 4 ("but not on this road"; 13:12, 13:18;
Haran = road). Readers' sheet: GR 39:7 given whole (the wicked called dead) and
Rashi on 15:15 (Terah repented); Ramban on *lek-ləḵā* as plain idiom; Zohar vs
